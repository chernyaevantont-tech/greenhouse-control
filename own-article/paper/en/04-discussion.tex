\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why physically motivated features can hurt}
\label{sec:disc-mechanism}

The physically motivated regressors used here---saturation vapour pressure
$e_s(T_{\text{in}})$, vapour-pressure deficit $D$ and screen-attenuated
radiation $I(1-u_{\text{ThScr}})$---are algebraic functions of variables the raw
library already contains: no new excitation, only smooth near-collinear copies
of existing columns. Table~\ref{tab:disc-libraries} shows the cost, measured on
the held-out identification block rather than on the full ladder of
Section~\ref{sec:res-ladder}, so that the same contrast can be read in-sample and
out. In that block those three
features raise the condition number of the regression from $8.2$ to $25.4$ and
the four cross terms raise it to $56.2$; one-step error falls monotonically
along that direction, $1.84 \rightarrow 1.64$~\textdegree C, while every
multi-step criterion moves the other way---median 24~h rollout error
$2.62 \rightarrow 21.88$~\textdegree C, divergence $0.000 \rightarrow 0.078$.
The ladder block quoted in Section~\ref{sec:res-ladder} is a different sample of
fits (20 replicates on both training years rather than 10 per fit year) and gives
the same three libraries as $\kappa = 8.2$, $24.5$ and $53.4$; the two sets of
figures must not be mixed within one comparison.

The chain is short. Collinearity leaves the least-squares solution weakly
determined along the ill-conditioned directions, coefficients become unstable
across identification replicates, and a fixed sparsity threshold then arbitrates
between near-equivalent representations---inconsistently from replicate to
replicate. In the cleanest one-factor contrast available, two controllers
differing in the library alone at identical optimiser and threshold, the boiler
coefficient $\xi_{u\text{Boil}}$ survives in 11 of 20 replicates with the raw
library and in 3 of 20 with the physics library without cross terms.
%% Survival 0.55 vs 0.15: regen/results/n7/main_n7.csv and
%% regen/results/priced_main/*.csv (sindy_mpc_raw_ens vs sindy_mpc_conf).
%% Survival is a property of the fit, so n = 20 seeds, not 80 runs.
The actuator term is squeezed into collinear feature combinations that cannot
substitute for it in a free-running simulation, and the error compounds over the
horizon.

That squeeze is real but it is not the carrier of the effect, and the priced
recomputation is what shows it. Reinstating the deleted coefficient stays
significant under the corrected objective (16 of 20 replicates improved,
Holm-corrected $p=1.2\times10^{-3}$), but its median effect is
$+0.21$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ against $+3.05$ under the defective one---a fourteenfold
collapse---and its mean, $+1.92$, is nine times its median, so the median is the
honest summary (Figure~\ref{fig:disc-knockin}).
%% Knock-in: regen/results/priced_mech/mechanism_pricedMech*.csv (405 rows, 5
%% duplicates removed on (block, condition, seed, test_year)) against
%% regen/results/final/mechanism*.csv.  Single season, test_year = 2020.
The same reading survives the decomposition over (library) $\times$ (term
retained): the raw library \emph{without} the term ($+3.48$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$) still
beats the physics library \emph{with} it ($+2.36$). That split is
observational---survival is a property of each fit, not a randomised factor, and
it is confounded with recipe---so it bounds the library effect rather than
isolating the term.
%% 2x2 cells: priced pool, controllers grouped by feature_variant per
%% regen/regen_config.py.  Survival is constant across the four seasons of a
%% seed, so the effective n is 19 and 20 seeds, not 76 and 320 runs.
The cross-term contrasts add nothing under either objective (all six
non-significant, two of three estimates changing sign), so no directional claim
follows.

\subsection{A cheap open-loop rule for selecting a control-oriented surrogate}
\label{sec:disc-rule}

The rule follows from the same measurements: score candidates open-loop on (i)
free-running multi-step rollout error at a horizon no shorter than the one the
model will be used at, (ii) the fraction of rollouts that diverge and (iii) the
condition number of the regression---all computed on the identification data in
seconds, before any closed-loop season is simulated---and keep one-step error
out. Ours scored 96 steps (24~h) against a 20-step (5~h) control horizon---a
stress test rather than a matched one, so the screening horizon has to be
reported with the score. In our ladder the
raw configuration dominates the frozen physics-informed recipe on all three
pre-registered axes at once (Section~\ref{sec:res-ladder}), and the two
controllers built on that library then take the top two places in closed loop.
The frozen recipe leads on exactly one metric, one-step accuracy (rank 8 of 72
against rank 10), which was not a gate. The screen is close to free: the whole
ladder cell costs a few seconds per fit, against a simulated season per candidate
for a closed-loop evaluation.
%% Ladder ranks and values: regen/results/ladder_rerun/ladder_rerun.csv +
%% ladder_rerun2.csv (1440 rows = 20 seeds x 72 configs, zero duplicates).
%% Screen cost: `secs` column, 3.2 s (raw/d1/stlsq/none) and 3.8 s (frozen).
%% Caution on "72": the `constrained` and `sr3` labels are bit-identical in all
%% 360 seed-cells, so the grid holds 54 distinct fits under 72 labels.  Ranks 8
%% and 10 are unaffected (the first tied pair sits at rank 11).

Two qualifications. The ladder's rollout value is a \emph{comparator}, not a
specification: along the oracle controller's own closed-loop trajectory the same
frozen recipe predicts indoor temperature to $2.62$~\textdegree C at 24~h, four
times better than the $10.50$~\textdegree C it scores on PRBS-excited
identification data.
%% regen/results/v3_parity/parity_v3.csv (120 rows, 5 seeds, season 2020).
The held-out block supports the ordering but licenses a different claim: the
ordering is identical in-sample and held-out, yet the dominant factor is the
\emph{evaluation} year. The physics library without cross terms scores a median
rollout error of $16.9$~\textdegree C evaluated on 2018
and $2.7$~\textdegree C on 2019 whatever year it was fitted on, and in one of
the four fit$\rightarrow$evaluate directions it narrowly beats the raw library
($2.687$ against $2.722$). What is defensible is that the raw library is stable
regardless of evaluation year while the physics libraries are year-dependent.
Physical motivation is therefore not evidence that a basis function belongs in
the library; what matters is whether it adds information beyond the columns
already present, and here it does not.

\subsection{Sparsity is exonerated}
\label{sec:disc-sparsity}

The winner is the sparser model on both counts---11 candidate features against
14, and $19.9$ retained terms against $28.25$---so sparsity cannot be what the
physics-informed variants lost. Nor is more sparsity better within a library:
over the sparsity sweep of Table~\ref{tab:lambda} margin falls as terms are
removed, and violation steps rise with it, from about $3850$ to $9090$ between
the endpoints. The decline is not monotone across the thirteen levels, and
the between-replicate SD reaches $4.14$, larger than several of the steps, so
only the endpoints carry the comparison.
%% regen/results/priced_mech/mechanism_pricedMech*.csv, lambda sweep block,
%% n = 20 per level, test_year = 2020 only.  Level means, lambda ascending:
%% 6.577 6.575 6.107 6.376 6.414 4.302 4.039 3.592 4.010 3.037 3.113 4.606
%% 2.433; SD 1.25 1.23 2.52 2.00 1.82 3.51 4.14 2.96 2.37 1.09 0.36 0.93 2.49.
Term count orders performance in neither direction; the basis does. The two
raw-library variants---bagged ensemble and plain sequential thresholded least
squares---cannot be separated in closed loop (median $\Delta=-0.01$, mean
$+0.48$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, $33/80$ wins, $p=0.60$), which locates the effect in the
library rather than in the estimator. This is a failure to reject, not a
demonstration of equivalence: the mean gap is not negligible, only too disperse
to establish at $n=80$.

\subsection{What the two comparison defects imply for the field}
\label{sec:disc-defects}

Both defects (Table~\ref{tab:disc-defects}, Figure~\ref{fig:disc-corrections})
were on our side of the comparison and both inflated the result.

The reference heuristic was described as tuned and was not. Searching its four
economically relevant setpoints under exactly the budget the reinforcement-learning
agents received---16 random trials, selection on the training years only---is worth
$+3.49$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ and moves the heuristic from last-but-three to fifth of
thirteen. The apparent advantage of the raw-library controller over it falls
from $+5.54$ to $+2.06$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$: an untuned baseline had inflated the
result $2.7$-fold.
%% Both figures use the SAME harness for the reference: stock_test and
%% tuned_test in regen/results/n2_tune/tune_rb_n2.csv (-1.2264 and +2.2613,
%% 1 deterministic run per season) against sindy_mpc_raw_ens under the priced
%% objective (+4.3173, 80 runs).  5.5437 - 3.4878 = 2.0560 exactly.  The
%% canonical rule_based rows in final/main.csv give the same contrast as
%% +5.5234 (paired, n = 80, p = 1.2e-14); the 0.02 difference is the
%% cross-harness drift disclosed in Section 4.6.  Do not mix the two.
Only 2 of the 16 random draws beat the stock setpoints: the
stock configuration sat at the 88th percentile of its own search space---not a
straw man, merely un-searched. Tuning also moved the heuristic \emph{onto} the
two-axis front, where untuned it had been dominated.
%% Default-objective front with the tuned heuristic included: sindy_mpc_raw_ens
%% (+4.07, 4467), rule_based_tuned (+2.26, 4339), ppo (+0.45, 1330).  The stock
%% heuristic (-1.23, 2813) is dominated by ppo and by rule_based.
Tuning budgets should be reported in units comparable to those granted to the
learned controllers~\cite{henderson2018}.

The second defect was internal to the controller. The MPC stage cost weighted
boiler, lamp and CO$_2$ in the ratio $1:0.5:0.1$, while the marginal costs the
economic criterion actually charges are $1:2.97:0.46$: the boiler was penalised
twice as hard as the lamp although the lamp costs three times more. Re-weighting
the energy term to the measured ratios, its total at $u=(1,1,1)$ held fixed,
improved \emph{both} axes for all eight MPC controllers, but unevenly: the five
physics-informed variants gained $+1.54\ldots+2.74$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ and the
neural-network MPC $+1.57$, against $+0.21\ldots+0.25$ for the two raw ones.
%% Per-controller deltas (priced - default, seed- and season-matched):
%% dense +2.738, lowthr +2.676, conf_dagger +1.777, dense_dagger +1.733,
%% nn_mpc +1.565, conf +1.538, raw_ens +0.246, raw +0.214.  Sources:
%% priced_main + priced_dagger against final/main.csv, except the two raw
%% variants whose only default-objective runs are in n7/main_n7.csv.
Taking the best physics-informed variant, the raw-minus-physics gap fell from
$+3.66$ to $+1.23$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$: roughly two-thirds of what had looked like a
library effect was a defect of the objective. The repair also cost the tuned
heuristic its place on the two-axis front, yielded to a physics-informed variant
it had beaten under the defective objective---a baseline's standing in a
two-axis comparison is no more stable than the stage cost the controllers were
given. Why the correction is worth an order of magnitude more to one library
than to the other we did not measure; what follows practically is that a
controller defect can masquerade as a property of the model class, and that the
stage cost should be checked against the prices in the evaluation criterion
before any difference is attributed to a model.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:disc-related}

The finding instantiates the identification-for-control programme---model
quality cannot be judged independently of the control task the model
serves~\cite{gevers1993,vandenhof1995,ljung1991,hjalmarsson2005,controlorientedsurvey2025}---and
the objective-mismatch literature of model-based reinforcement learning, where
prediction likelihood is a poor proxy for control
performance~\cite{lambert2020,wei2023,farahmand2017,jackson2022}. Our
contribution there is constructive rather than diagnostic: an open-loop screen
costing seconds per candidate reproduces the closed-loop ordering, whereas the
standard screen reproduces it \emph{inverted}. The screen needs only
free-running rollouts, so it transfers in principle to any model class; but we
exercised it on sparse-regression libraries and carried only two of the three
ranked libraries into closed loop, so the correspondence is established over two
libraries, not over model classes.
Section~\ref{sec:disc-defects} adds a second, less-studied mismatch: stage-cost
weights against the prices in the criterion, inside an otherwise correctly
specified problem. The mechanism measured here---collinear regressors inflating
the condition number and destabilising sparse estimates---is what reweighted and
balance-guided sparse regression is designed to
mitigate~\cite{cortiella2021,balanceguided2026,belsley1980}, which argues that
library \emph{design} deserves the scrutiny usually reserved for the estimator;
it converges from the opposite direction with library optimisation scored by
recursive long-term prediction~\cite{yonezawa2025}, while bagged
ensembling~\cite{fasel2022} did not rescue the ill-conditioned library here.
Greenhouse comparisons of MPC and reinforcement learning have generally fixed
the model and varied the
controller~\cite{morcego2023,mallick2024,vanlaatum2025,lin2021}; the library
appears at least as consequential.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{The advantage is not Pareto-dominant.} Five controllers hold the
front; PPO incurs $1330$ violation steps against the raw library's $4130$, at
$3.86$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ less margin. Superiority is conditional on how a grower
weights margin against corridor excursions.

\item \textbf{The MPC objective is necessarily a proxy.} The surrogate carries
no crop state, so revenue cannot be predicted and terminal biomass cannot be
priced---structural to the model class, and it blocks the natural terminal-cost
formulation.

\item \textbf{The tuned-heuristic comparison is not a paired test.} The
heuristic is deterministic and ran once per season, so its value is a constant
reused across replicates and carries no sampling uncertainty: the $+2.06$ above
is a one-sample signed-rank against that constant. Genuinely paired it collapses
to one replicate's four seasons ($+2.55$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, $n=4$), where the
minimum attainable $p$ is $0.125$ and nothing can be significant.
%% +2.06 = mean over all 80 raw_ens runs minus the per-season constant;
%% +2.55 = the same contrast restricted to seed 0, the only seed the heuristic
%% was run at (regen/results/n2_tune/tune_rb_n2.csv).

\item \textbf{Significance is not magnitude.} Two physics-informed variants
differ by $0.0066$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ over four seasons yet separate at $p=0.012$, so
listing both as front points is an artefact. Effect sizes accompany every
$p$-value, and Holm corrections are over a declared family of 13
controllers---another declaration moves them.

\item \textbf{Coverage is uneven.} The neural-network MPC has 10 replicates and
40 runs against 20 and 80 elsewhere, 14 of them terminating early. The oracle
has no 2022 season---all 20 runs exhausted the solver-failure budget---but that
is an artefact: at a thousandfold budget the season completes at $-1.12$ and
$-0.56$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, so the reported $-2.65$ is a three-season mean and the
imputed four-season value is $\approx-2.20$. One front member owes part of its
position to truncation: over complete runs its violation margin narrows from 71
to 26 steps.

\item \textbf{The mechanism block is single-season.} All 800 mechanism runs are
for 2020, so neither the $\lambda$ sweep nor the ablation generalises across
seasons. The knock-in and cross-term blocks rest on different baselines
(boiler-term survival $0.15$ against $1.00$) and are not mutually comparable;
the split by term survival is observational and confounded with recipe, and only
the knock-in contrast is controlled.

\item \textbf{The held-out check rests on two training years.} With 2018 and
2019 alone, year-dependence cannot be separated from chance, and the evaluation
year dominates (Section~\ref{sec:disc-rule}).

\item \textbf{The design and sensitivity blocks were never run under the priced
objective.} The support harness hard-codes the default stage cost, so those
sensitivities are default-objective results, mislabelled as priced in the run
log. Under it, perturbing identified coefficients by $\pm15\,\%$ and $\pm20\,\%$
degrades margin significantly (9 of 10 and 10 of 10 replicates worse, span of
means $12.2$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$), but as a growing minority of catastrophic runs, not
a uniform decline---the median at $\pm20\,\%$ is only $-0.31$. This finer grid
stops at $\pm20\,\%$, so the non-monotonicity the coarser canonical grid shows
at $\pm30\,\%$ is unreproduced rather than refuted.

\item \textbf{The horizon was never tuned for any MPC.} Every MPC improves at
horizon 8 over horizon 20, by $+0.29$ for the raw library and
$+1.3\ldots+2.1$ for the physics-informed variants---consistent with the
compounding-error mechanism, and an asymmetry running against the MPCs, which
received no hyperparameter search while the reinforcement learning agents and
the heuristic received 16 trials each.

\item \textbf{Reproducibility is within-environment only.} The pipeline
reproduces nine digests bit-for-bit on one machine, but closed-loop margins are
not stable across machines, and the drift is visible inside the present data
even for the deterministic heuristic: the two harnesses score it differently in
all four seasons at identical configuration hash, by up to
$0.038$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ and monotonically more with each season.
%% Heuristic drift: rule_based in regen/results/final/main.csv (+4.6335,
%% -6.9712, +1.3275, -3.8143) against stock_test in n2_tune/tune_rb_n2.csv
%% (+4.6304, -6.9846, +1.3009, -3.8524).
The stronger cross-platform figures quoted in Section~\ref{sec:repro} carry an
[UNSOURCED] marker there, because the second machine's wave is not under
\texttt{regen/results} and cannot be recomputed; the in-tree harness drift above
is what carries this limitation, and one decision---restore that wave or cut
those figures---settles both places.
Whole waves must be regenerated in one named environment; the configuration
hash does not discriminate between variants.

\item \textbf{Negative results stand as such.} The out-of-distribution guard
does not pay for itself, and the sign of its effect depends on which statistic is
taken --- mean against median, all pairs against completed seasons
(Section~\ref{sec:res-safety}). It truncates a left tail rather than raising the
centre, costs violations, ends $30\,\%$ of its seasons early, and rests on a
detector barely better than chance; its activation count was never recorded, so
the natural explanatory variable is missing. The extended Kalman filter observer
is worse than doing nothing ($-2.95$ against $-1.58$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$), $39\,\%$ of
its seasons terminating early.

\item \textbf{Scope.} Simulation only, one location, one planting date, one crop
model, one weather source. The ranking is not price-invariant: two different
controllers win across a nine-point grid of fruit and energy prices---though
that grid re-scores rather than re-optimises and contains neither raw-library
controller, so it does not test the ranking reported here.
%% regen/results/final/tables/sensitivity_prices.csv: fruit_price in
%% {0.8, 1.6, 3.2} x energy_scale in {0.5, 1.0, 2.0}; winners rule_based 6/9,
%% sindy_mpc_dense_dagger 3/9; sindy_mpc_raw and sindy_mpc_raw_ens absent.
No recursive feasibility or stability guarantee is claimed or
proved~\cite{mayne2000}.

\end{itemize}
