\begin{abstract}
Economic model predictive control (MPC) can coordinate crop-climate requirements
and resource costs in greenhouse production, but comparing candidate process
models at crop scale is expensive. We therefore conducted an in silico study using
the published GreenLight tomato greenhouse model driven by ERA5 weather. Seventy-two
sparse-identification configurations were screened, and fifteen controllers were
compared over four 60-day test seasons with up to 20 identification replicates.
Outcomes were simulated seasonal margin and climate-corridor violations. One-step
accuracy and multi-step stability ranked the feature libraries oppositely:
physically motivated features reduced one-step temperature RMSE from 1.86 to
1.68~\textdegree C but increased median 24~h rollout RMSE from 2.67 to
24.27~\textdegree C. The library selected by multi-step stability achieved the
highest mean margin ($+4.32$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$), compared with $+3.09$ for the best
physics-informed variant and $+2.26$ for a tuned agronomic heuristic. Conditioning
did not explain the non-monotone margin pattern ($+4.32$, $+0.28$, $+2.75$);
performance instead tracked survival of the direct boiler coefficient after
sparsification (55, 15, 55~\%). Five controllers remained on the margin--violation
Pareto front. These results support rollout-based, actuator-aware surrogate
screening for greenhouse MPC, but establish comparative simulation evidence rather
than field performance.
\end{abstract}

\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} greenhouse climate control; protected tomato production; economic model predictive
control; sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics; simulation-based evaluation;
control-oriented model selection; multi-step prediction error; actuator-pathway survival

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Protected tomato production depends on maintaining a productive microclimate while
containing the variable costs of heating, supplementary lighting and CO$_2$ enrichment.
These inputs affect crop growth and resource use simultaneously, so a control strategy
should be judged agronomically and economically rather than by setpoint tracking alone
\cite{katzin2020,katzin2021}. Economic model predictive control (MPC) provides that
framework: inputs are optimised over a finite horizon subject to temperature, humidity and
CO$_2$ constraints \cite{vanhenten1994,mayne2000,rawlings2012,lin2021}.

Simulation-based greenhouse studies are established in this application area. Xu et al.\
used a greenhouse--crop model to compare open- and closed-loop optimal control and to
estimate lettuce yield and profit in an explicitly computational study
\cite{xu2023agronomy}. Wen et al.\ combined measured blueberry photosynthesis with an SVR
model and simulated multi-objective light--CO$_2$ regulation
\cite{wen2022agronomy}, while E{\'c}im-{\DJ}uri{\'c} et al.\ coupled a TRNSYS greenhouse
model to an artificial neural network and anchored the thermal model to measured
temperatures \cite{ecimduric2024}. These examples show both the value and the evidential
limit of simulation: virtual experiments can isolate controller and model-design effects,
but field performance requires empirical anchoring. The present study is deliberately at
the simulation-only end of that spectrum. It uses the independently published GreenLight
tomato model and recorded weather forcing, but introduces no new greenhouse, plant or
actuator measurements. Its claims are therefore comparative statements inside a defined
virtual testbed, not demonstrations of commercial greenhouse performance.

MPC requires a process model, and calibrating a detailed mechanistic greenhouse model for
every site is laborious. A surrogate identified from simulated or measured trajectories
can reduce that burden. Sparse identification of nonlinear dynamics (SINDy) is attractive
because it expresses the dynamics as a sparse combination of library functions
\cite{brunton2016,brunton2016c,kaiser2018,fasel2022}. Unlike a neural surrogate, the
resulting equations can be inspected for physical and dimensional consistency and embedded
analytically in an MPC solver.

Three benchmarking risks complicate an objective comparison of such surrogates. First,
learned controllers may be compared with an unsearched heuristic even though tuned
agronomic rules are a strong reference \cite{morcego2023,mallick2025,vanlaatum2025}.
Second, a setpoint-tracking index may be reported instead of economic margin
\cite{lin2021,morcego2023}, although the two need not be monotonically related. Third,
surrogate hyperparameters are commonly selected by one-step prediction accuracy
\cite{kaiser2018,yonezawa2026}, implicitly assuming that the best short-horizon predictor
also gives the best controller. Table~\ref{tab:defects} states the safeguards used here
and quantifies sensitivity to removing them.

The third assumption is precisely what identification-for-control research warns against:
a model should be judged by the loop it closes, not by open-loop fit alone
\cite{ljung1999,gevers1993,vandenhof1995}. In model-based reinforcement learning the same
problem is termed \emph{objective mismatch}: predictive accuracy can be a poor proxy for
control performance \cite{lambert2020,jackson2022,farahmand2017}, and recent surveys show
that it remains relevant for modern data-driven surrogates
\cite{wei2024,controlorientedsurvey2025}. Exhaustive closed-loop screening is expensive
even in simulation because it requires a solver and a full cropping season per candidate.
The practical question is therefore narrower: if a greenhouse surrogate must be selected
open loop, which inexpensive criterion best anticipates seasonal closed-loop economics?

A related question concerns feature-library design. Physically motivated basis functions
such as saturation vapour pressure, vapour-pressure deficit and screen-attenuated
radiation are often included on the reasonable assumption that known physics can only
help \cite{raissi2019,karniadakis2021}. Yet these functions are nonlinear transforms of
the same climate states and may be nearly collinear with them; collinearity can destabilise
sparse regression \cite{cortiella2021,balanceguided2026}. We therefore test, rather than
assume, whether physically motivated features improve a control-oriented surrogate.

We address both questions in a controlled in silico experiment using the published
GreenLight tomato model \cite{katzin2020,vanlaatum2025}, ERA5-derived weather for one
location and a 15-min control step. Candidate surrogates were identified on two training
seasons, and fifteen controllers were evaluated --- nine SINDy-MPC variants, a
neural-network MPC, two deep reinforcement-learning agents
\cite{schulman2017,haarnoja2018,raffin2021},
a full-simulator-model MPC and the rule-based heuristic in both
its stock and tuned configurations --- over four held-out test seasons. A \emph{season}
here is the 60-day window from 1 March, and the outcome is model-derived margin per square
metre of greenhouse floor area. Each learned controller was run at 20 identification
replicates per season (10 for
the neural-network MPC); the heuristic is deterministic and was run once per season, and
the full-simulator-model MPC returned only three of the four seasons, its 2022 runs having
exhausted the solver-failure budget. Comparisons use non-parametric signed-rank tests with
Holm correction \cite{wilcoxon1945,holm1979,demsar2006}; against the deterministic
heuristic the test is one-sample against a constant rather than genuinely paired. The
contributions are four.

\textbf{(i) One-step accuracy and multi-step stability rank candidate libraries in
opposite orders, and the closed-loop ordering follows the multi-step one.} The
identification ladder evaluated 72 labelled configurations, covering 54 distinct fits ---
two of the optimizer labels turn out to be bit-identical. Within its degree-1 block ---
the degree-2 candidates are all non-embeddable in the MPC solver --- the library with the
best one-step accuracy is the worst on every multi-step metric. Taking the undenoised
cells under the two sparse estimators ($n = 40$ fits per library), adding physically
motivated features improves
one-step RMSE from $1.86$ to $1.68$~$^\circ$C while degrading the median 24-h rollout RMSE
from $2.67$ to $24.27$~$^\circ$C, raising the regression condition number from $8.2$ to
$53.4$ and the divergence rate from $0.000$ to $0.076$ (Figure~\ref{fig:kappa});
withholding only the bilinear cross terms recovers part of the loss but not the ordering
(rollout $10.58$~$^\circ$C, $\kappa = 24.5$). In closed loop, a raw, physics-free library
--- also the sparsest of the three --- attains the best
four-season mean margin of the fifteen controllers, $+4.32$~EUR~m$^{-2}$ against $+3.09$
for the best physics-informed variant, ahead in 62 of 80 season~$\times$~replicate pairs
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 4.6 \times 10^{-4}$, family of 15). The gap is small against the
run-to-run spread --- standard deviations $4.08$ and $2.71$ for a difference of $1.23$ ---
and it reverses in one of the four seasons ($-1.56$ against $+0.27$ in 2021). The library
ordering holds again on four seasons never used at any stage of model or controller
development (four controllers, eight replicates, default objective). Within this virtual
testbed, physical motivation alone is therefore not sufficient evidence that a basis
function belongs in the control-oriented library.

\textbf{(ii) The effect of physically motivated features is non-monotone.} All three
libraries were carried into closed loop under a
strict one-factor design --- same estimator, same sparsity threshold, same seeds, same
priced objective, four seasons $\times$ 20 replicates, zero truncated runs, the library
the only thing that changes. Open-loop degradation is monotone in the number of physics
features: $\kappa = 8.2 \rightarrow 24.5 \rightarrow 53.4$ and median rollout
$2.67 \rightarrow 10.58 \rightarrow 24.27$~$^\circ$C. Closed-loop margin is not:
$+4.32 \rightarrow +0.28 \rightarrow +2.75$~EUR~m$^{-2}$. The worst-conditioned library
beats the middle one by $+2.47$ on average (median $+2.39$, 63 of 80 paired runs,
$p = 1.3\times10^{-8}$), in each of the four seasons taken separately. Under the second
sparse estimator only the two endpoints exist as closed-loop controllers ($+3.83$ raw,
$+2.48$ full physics), so the raw-over-physics direction replicates there but the
three-point non-monotonicity rests on one estimator. Conditioning therefore predicts
open-loop multi-step stability cleanly but is not a sufficient explanation of closed-loop
economics; contribution~(iv) identifies the controller-facing quantity that better tracks
the outcome.

\textbf{(iii) Benchmarking safeguards materially change the apparent effect size.}
Against the tuned heuristic the raw-library controller is better by
$+2.06$~EUR~m$^{-2}$, ahead in 75 of 80 season~$\times$~replicate comparisons
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 6.3 \times 10^{-11}$, Holm correction over a family of 15). Three
further SINDy-MPC variants also clear
the tuned heuristic significantly, by smaller margins ($+1.57$, $+0.83$, $+0.82$), and the
two full-\texttt{physics} controllers are ahead of it in point estimate without being
separable from it: the raw library is first, not alone. Against the unsearched reference
the same advantage would read $+5.54$, demonstrating the sensitivity of the headline
comparison to baseline tuning. Aligning actuator weights with the evaluation price vector
likewise reduces the raw-minus-physics gap from $+3.66$ to $+1.23$
(Table~\ref{tab:defects}, row 2).

\textbf{(iv) What the closed-loop outcome does track is survival of the actuator
pathway through the sparsity threshold.} Across the one-factor triple, the direct boiler
coefficient $\xi_{u\mathrm{Boil}}$ is retained in 11 of 20 identification replicates with
the raw library, 11 of 20 with full physics and 3 of 20 with physics-without-cross-terms
--- $55\,\%$, $55\,\%$, $15\,\%$, which is the margin ordering and not the $\kappa$
ordering. A surrogate whose boiler column has been thresholded away cannot be steered
with the boiler, however accurate it is one step ahead and however well conditioned the
fit that produced it. The controlled evidence is a single-coefficient ablation, the only
randomised manipulation in the mechanism block: reinstating the deleted coefficient in
the frozen recipe, with the library, the scalers, every other term and the MPC
construction held fixed, is positive in 16 of 20 replicates
($p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 1.2 \times 10^{-3}$ over the two knock tests) with median
$+0.21$~EUR~m$^{-2}$ and mean
$+1.92$ --- significant, typically small and heavily right-skewed --- while the
complementary knock-out is null. We are explicit about the limits of this account.
Survival \emph{gates} the outcome but does not \emph{order} it: among the two libraries
that keep the term the raw one still wins by $+1.56$~EUR~m$^{-2}$, and raw-library runs
that lost the term still average $+3.48$, above physics-library runs that kept it
($+2.36$). The obvious structural account of why the middle library is the worst ---
that it carries enough collinearity for the threshold to destabilise the direct boiler
term but, unlike full physics, has no bilinear $T_{\mathrm{in}}u_{\mathrm{Boil}}$ term to
carry the heating pathway once the direct one is cut --- has been tested and rejected:
deleting exactly that one feature from the full library leaves conditioning
($\kappa = 52.3$), open-loop instability and closed-loop margin ($+2.11$~EUR~m$^{-2}$,
$p_{\mathrm{Holm}} = 4.4 \times 10^{-6}$ above the middle library) at the full library's
level, with survival at $40\,\%$ --- nowhere near the collapse a bilinear bypass predicts
(Sections~\ref{sec:res-mech} and~\ref{sec:discussion}). Why one library loses the term is
thus unresolved; that survival gates the outcome is not.

What this does not show is equally explicit. The advantage is not Pareto-dominant: the
front holds five points, and the PPO agent attains far fewer constraint violations
($1330$ against $4130$ steps per season) at a lower margin. The surrogate carries no crop
state, so the MPC objective is necessarily a proxy and terminal biomass cannot be priced.
The mechanism experiments are single-season and replication is unequal across controllers.
The non-monotonicity rests on the middle library, which reaches closed loop under one
estimator only, and survival is measured rather than manipulated at library level --- the
ablation is the only randomised contrast anywhere in the mechanism, and the term-deletion
test that removed the detour explanation is, like the library comparison itself, a
controlled structural comparison rather than a randomised one.
All results are simulation-only, for one location and one planting date, and
reproducibility is established within one computing environment only. Consequently, the
reported margins and rankings are comparative computational evidence, not estimates of
field-scale benefit or recommendations for direct commercial deployment.
The remainder of
the paper is organised as follows. Section~\ref{sec:methods} describes the controlled
object, the economic criterion, the data, the controllers and the identification
protocol; Section~\ref{sec:results} reports the model-selection ladder, the four-season
economic comparison and the mechanism experiments; Section~\ref{sec:discussion} discusses
the implications and the limits; Section~\ref{sec:conclusions} concludes.

\section{Materials and Methods}

\subsection{Simulation study design, site, weather and cropping season}
\label{sec:sim}

This study is an in silico comparative experiment: no new greenhouse sensor records,
plant measurements, harvested-yield observations or actuator trials were collected. All
controller development and evaluation used the published GreenLight tomato greenhouse
model \cite{katzin2020,katzin2021} through the GreenLight-Gym interface
\cite{vanlaatum2025}. GreenLight provides a common mechanistic plant--climate testbed, so
controller and surrogate choices can be changed while the virtual crop and greenhouse are
held fixed. It does not remove model-form error or the simulation-to-reality gap; those
limits constrain the interpretation in Section~\ref{sec:disc-limitations}.

The simulated house is at Rostov-on-Don ($47.24^{\circ}$~N, $39.71^{\circ}$~E). Weather
forcing came from the ERA5/ERA5-Land reanalysis \cite{hersbach2020} via Open-Meteo
\cite{openmeteo2023}; sky temperature was derived from the retrieved dew point and cloud
cover, and the deep-soil boundary condition was refitted as a single annual harmonic to
ERA5-Land soil temperature at this site, replacing the simulator's Dutch default.

%% Open-Meteo Historical archive API over ERA5/ERA5-Land, hourly temperature_2m,
%% relative_humidity_2m, dew_point_2m, wind_speed_10m, shortwave_radiation and cloud_cover,

Outdoor CO$_2$ was held at 400~ppm, a limitation of the model. Each season spans
60~days from 1~March at a 15-min control step ($T_s=900$~s), i.e.\ 5760 control steps
per season; that count is the truncation gate used throughout.

Years were separated to prevent leakage: identification and
every selection decision used 2018--2019 only, closed-loop evaluation used 2020--2023 (2020 in-distribution,
2021--2023 out-of-distribution), and a further block of four seasons, 2014--2017, was
generated after all recipes had been frozen and entered no stage of training, selection
or tuning. Training trajectories were collected by the rule-based controller with a
pseudo-random binary excitation of amplitude 0.3 on its actions plus an additive
perturbation of scale 0.1; the condition number of the regression matrix was recorded
for every fit \cite{cortiella2021}. Evaluation rollouts carry no excitation.

\subsection{Economic criterion}
\label{sec:epi}

Controllers are scored by the profit term of the simulator's own reward function; no
tracking-error proxy is used. At step $k$ that term is a marginal income $\pi_k$, and the
seasonal economic performance indicator (EPI), written $J$ throughout, is
%
\begin{equation}
J=\sum_{k}\pi_k=\sum_{k}\bigl(g_k-c_k\bigr)\qquad\text{(1)},
\end{equation}
%
where $g_k$ is income from modelled fruit dry-matter growth, converted to fresh mass
with a dry-to-fresh ratio of 0.065 and valued at 1.60~EUR/kg, and $c_k$ are the
variable costs of heating (0.09~EUR/(kW$\cdot$h)), supplementary lighting
(0.30~EUR/(kW$\cdot$h)) and CO$_2$ enrichment (0.30~EUR/kg). Dividing the logged cost
columns by the logged energy and CO$_2$ columns of
\texttt{regen/results/final/main.csv} returns exactly these three prices across all 780
retained runs. $J$ is \emph{not} the scalar the environment returns as its reward: that
scalar rescales the profit and subtracts normalised corridor-violation penalties. The
distinction matters, because the two reinforcement-learning controllers are trained on the
penalised reward while every controller is scored on the unpenalised $J$ --- their position
on the violation axis partly reflects optimising a different objective from the one
reported.

What $J$ excludes matters for every result that follows. It omits planting material,
labour, water, fertiliser, depreciation and fixed costs; it uses modelled dry-matter
growth rather than realised marketable yield; and, critically, it does not value the
biomass standing in the house at the end of the 60-day window. The surrogates of
Section~\ref{sec:sindy} carry a three-component climate state and no crop state, so
terminal biomass cannot be priced inside the optimisation even in principle. That is why
the MPC stage cost (Section~\ref{sec:mpc}) is a proxy rather than a discretisation of
Equation~(1), and why $J$ is reported as a \emph{margin} and not as a farm
profit: a controller that harvests within the window is rewarded over one that builds a
crop for a later window, and no result here separates the two.

$J$ carries no penalty for leaving the productive range, so margin can be bought with
violations. The simulator's productive band is CO$_2\in[300,1600]$~ppm, air temperature
$[15,34]\,^\circ$C and relative humidity $[50,85]\,\%$. Controllers are therefore
compared by Pareto dominance in two axes --- $J$ and the seasonal count of steps in
violation --- and no scalarised ``margin-minus-penalty'' criterion is introduced,
because the simulator does not express violations in money. This corridor is also not the
one the predictive controllers minimise against: their stage cost (Section~\ref{sec:mpc})
penalises humidity only \emph{above} 85\,\% and CO$_2$ only \emph{below} a floor, so the
lower humidity and upper CO$_2$ bounds are unweighted in the optimisation yet counted on
the violation axis.

\subsection{Controllers compared}
\label{sec:controllers}

Fifteen controllers were compared (Table~\ref{tab:controllers}). The
\emph{low-threshold} SINDy variant is not a reduced first-principles grey box; it is the
same sparse estimator on the same feature library at a threshold of $10^{-6}$, i.e.\ the
least sparse member of the family. Second, the aggregation loop applied to two SINDy
controllers rolls the learner's own MPC in closed loop on the training seasons, appends
the visited states to the identification set and re-estimates the model (3~iterations of
5-day episodes). The rule-based controller is \emph{never queried} in that loop: this is
on-policy re-identification, not DAgger and not imitation learning, and the rule-based
controller is not an expert in it. One asymmetry inside that loop survives into the
headline comparison and is disclosed here: its episodes are rolled under the default stage
cost, so under the priced objective these two controllers aggregate their training states
under one cost function and are evaluated under another.

\subsection{Feature libraries and sparse identification}
\label{sec:sindy}

Sparse regression \cite{brunton2016,brunton2016c,kaiser2018} recovers an explicit
discrete one-step map
%
\begin{equation}
x_{k+1}=\Xi\,\Theta(x_k,u_k,d_k)\qquad\text{(2)},
\end{equation}
%
with state $x_k=(T_{\mathrm{in}},c,\varphi)^{\top}$ (air temperature, CO$_2$, relative
humidity), six controls $u_k$ (boiler, CO$_2$ injection, thermal screen, ventilation,
lamps, blackout screen) normalised to $[0,1]$, and disturbances $d_k$ (outdoor
temperature, global radiation, outdoor CO$_2$, two diurnal harmonics). Three feature
libraries were compared, differing only in $\Theta$:

\begin{itemize}
\item \texttt{raw} --- 11 features: the five disturbance channels and the six controls,
      with no state transform whatsoever;
\item \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} --- those 11 plus three physically motivated transforms
      of state, radiation and screen position (14 features): saturation vapour pressure
      $e_s(T_{\mathrm{in}})=0.6108\exp\bigl(17.27\,T_{\mathrm{in}}/(T_{\mathrm{in}}+237.3)\bigr)$
      by the Magnus--Tetens relation, vapour-pressure deficit $D=(1-\varphi/100)\,e_s$,
      and screen-attenuated radiation $S_{\mathrm{eff}}=I\,(1-u_{\mathrm{thscr}})$;
\item \texttt{physics} --- those 14 plus four bilinear state\,$\times$\,control cross
      terms ($T_{\mathrm{in}}S_{\mathrm{eff}}$, $\varphi u_{\mathrm{vent}}$,
      $(c-c_{\mathrm{out}})u_{\mathrm{vent}}$, $T_{\mathrm{in}}u_{\mathrm{boil}}$),
      18 features.
\end{itemize}

The three physical functions are nonlinear transforms of variables already in the raw
library: they add no new measurement, only near-collinear columns. States and features
are standardised before thresholding, so the threshold acts on dimensionless quantities,
and the coefficients are transformed back to physical units. Four estimators were
available: STLSQ, SR3, a constrained variant, and an ensemble optimiser with 20 bagged
bootstrap replicates \cite{fasel2022}. Because the ensemble re-draws its bootstrap, a
single fit is one realisation of a lottery; the draw is therefore carried as an explicit
design axis, and the number of draws is recorded per row rather than assumed --- 10 per
seed in the canonical wave, 6 per seed in the later wave that added the raw-library
controller.

\subsection{Identification ladder and pre-registered open-loop criteria}
\label{sec:ladder}

The identification recipe was selected in an open-loop ladder computed on the training
years alone: 3 libraries $\times$ 2 polynomial degrees $\times$ 4 estimators $\times$
3 denoisers $=72$ configuration labels, each fitted on 20 seeds, for 1440 fits
(\texttt{regen/results/ladder\_rerun/}). No closed-loop quantity enters it. Each fit
records the one-step RMSE, the multi-step rollout RMSE and divergence fraction at
horizons of 4, 20 and 96 steps (1~h, 5~h, 24~h; the reported value is always the 24-h
figure), the number of non-zero terms, the regression condition number, and whether the
map can be embedded analytically in the MPC. The pre-registration declared two gates,
MPC-embeddability and a sign-and-dimension transparency check, and specified multi-step
stability only qualitatively, as a divergence fraction near zero. What was applied differs
on both counts and is reported as applied: the divergence criterion was operationalised as
a fixed threshold of 0.05 in the table generator, and the transparency column is empty in
all 1440 rows, so that gate was never evaluated and is not claimed here. Under
embeddability and the 0.05 threshold, 32 of the 72 labels pass.
Figure~\ref{fig:kappa}a shows the degree-1 undenoised block of this grid in the plane of
the two prediction criteria, with the 0.05 gate encoded by marker fill.

Four properties of this grid bound what it can support. (i)~The rollout metrics are
computed on the same 2018--2019 data the model was fitted to and are therefore
in-sample; a separate held-out block (240 fits: 10 seeds $\times$ 3 libraries $\times$
2 estimators $\times$ 4 fit-year~$\rightarrow$~eval-year directions,
\texttt{regen/results/holdout/}) splits the two training years to test whether the
library ordering survives. (ii)~The \texttt{constrained} and \texttt{sr3} estimators
produce bit-identical fits in all 360 seed-cells, so the ladder holds 54 distinct
configurations under 72 labels and pooled per-library averages double-weight that
estimator. (iii)~Every degree-2 configuration is non-embeddable, so the embeddability
gate is exactly the degree-1 filter here and carries no independent information.
(iv)~All rollout horizons are supplied and audited in control steps rather than calendar
days; the reported ladder is the step-indexed recomputation used by every analysis here.

\subsection{MPC formulation and stage cost}
\label{sec:mpc}

The explicit form of Equation~(2) allows it to be embedded analytically in a
finite-horizon optimal control problem \cite{mayne2000}:
%
\begin{equation}
\min_{u_{0},\dots,u_{N-1}}\ \sum_{k=0}^{N-1}\bigl[\ell_{\mathrm{res}}(u_k)
+\ell_{\mathrm{corr}}(x_k)\bigr]+\ell_{\mathrm{corr}}(x_N)
\quad\text{s.t.}\quad x_{k+1}=\Xi\,\Theta(x_k,u_k,d_k),
\label{eq:mpc}
\end{equation}
%
with $N=20$ steps (5~h). The corridor term is
$\ell_{\mathrm{corr}}=30\,b_T^2+3\cdot10^{-4}b_{c}^2+8\,b_{\varphi}^2$, where $b_T$,
$b_c$ and $b_\varphi$ are excursions from a diurnally varying productive band:
temperature in $[15+3w,\,19+5w]\,^\circ$C, CO$_2$ above $400+300w$~ppm and relative
humidity below 85\,\%, with $w\in[0,1]$ a smooth day weight. Violations are penalised
softly so the problem stays feasible; temperature is hard-bounded to
$[12,35]\,^\circ$C, ventilation to $[0,0.4]$ and the other controls to $[0,1]$, and a
move-suppression term penalises control increments. The problem is solved by an
interior-point method (IPOPT \cite{wachter2006} through do-mpc \cite{fiedler2023}), whose
initial guess is set once from the measured initial state and carried forward by the
solver itself; no controller-specific warm start is supplied. The budget is 100 solver
failures, shared by every solver-based
controller. When a solve fails the step falls back to a fixed action (boiler 0.3, thermal
screen closed, all else zero) and the failure counts against that budget; a run that
exhausts it is excluded (Section~\ref{sec:stats}).

Two resource-weight variants were evaluated in $\ell_{\mathrm{res}}$. The default
implementation uses
$20u_{\mathrm{boil}}+10u_{\mathrm{lamp}}+2u_{\mathrm{CO}_2}$, a ratio of $1:0.5:0.1$.
Dividing the logged cost columns by the logged actuator sums in
\texttt{regen/results/final/main.csv} gives marginal costs per unit of accumulated
normalised control of 0.00293, 0.00870 and 0.00135~EUR for boiler, lamps and CO$_2$,
respectively, or $1:2.97:0.46$. The primary \texttt{priced} formulation therefore uses
$7.22u_{\mathrm{boil}}+21.44u_{\mathrm{lamp}}+3.33u_{\mathrm{CO}_2}$, scaled so the total
at $u=(1,1,1)$ is 31.99 against 32.0 under the default formulation. The corridor term is
unchanged, isolating the relative actuator weights while preserving the overall
resource--corridor balance.

The price-aligned formulation is used for the main closed-loop comparison and the
mechanism block. The supporting blocks on unseen seasons, shortened horizon,
bootstrap-draw count, design and coefficient sensitivity, adaptation, the safety guard and
fault injection were generated with the default formulation and are labelled accordingly.
They should be interpreted as robustness analyses of that formulation; coefficient and
threshold sensitivity have not been re-evaluated under the price-aligned weights.

\subsection{Statistical treatment}
\label{sec:stats}

Dispersion is generated by re-collecting the identification data in every repeat, so each
seed identifies a different surrogate; for the reinforcement-learning controllers the seed
also re-runs training. In the main closed-loop comparison twenty seeds were run for every
surrogate-based and learned controller; the supporting blocks use fewer, and their $n$ is
stated with each. Two exceptions are quoted wherever they appear: the neural MPC has 20
seeds under the default objective but only 10 under the priced one, and the idealised
controller loses one whole season to the exclusion rule below. The heuristic is
deterministic: the stock version was nevertheless repeated on all 20 seeds in the main wave,
returning 20 identical values per season, while the tuning wave ran the stock and tuned
versions once per season, so the tuned reference has $n=4$ and no seed axis at all.

Three accounting rules precede any average. First, several waves were resumed after
interruptions and their files overlap, so rows are deduplicated on
(method, seed, test\_year) --- or on the block-specific factor key --- before
aggregation; the deduplicated priced pool contains 600 rows. Second, a run is excluded
when it was truncated \emph{and} exhausted the solver-failure budget, which removes 20 of
the 800 rows of the canonical main table, all \texttt{oracle\_mpc} in 2022. Third,
seasons terminated by the simulator rather than the solver are retained as outcomes
(42~such seasons in the canonical table), and every headline figure is recomputed three
ways --- as-is, over complete seasons only, and normalised by the realised season
fraction --- with any rank change reported.

Paired comparisons use the Wilcoxon signed-rank test \cite{wilcoxon1945} with Holm
correction \cite{holm1979}. Because Holm's adjustment depends on the family, the family
size is stated with every adjusted $p$-value, and adjusted values from different families
are not comparable. One departure is fundamental rather than presentational: because both
rule-based references are deterministic, comparisons against either are \textbf{one-sample
signed-rank tests of the differences against a constant}, not genuinely paired tests, and
the reference carries no sampling uncertainty. The same limit binds any
test that treats a deterministic controller as the sample: at $n=4$ the minimum attainable
two-sided $p$ is 0.125, so such a comparison can never reach significance whatever the
effect size, and its point estimate is reported without a significance claim. An
across-season spread quoted for a deterministic controller is labelled as such and is not
an error bar.

Effect sizes accompany every $p$-value, with medians preferred to means wherever the
distribution is skewed, and bootstrap confidence intervals \cite{efron1994,demsar2006}
for aggregate quantities. This is deliberate: the data contain a contrast significant at
$p=0.012$ for a difference of 0.0066~EUR/m$^2$, and another whose mean is nine times its
median.

\subsection{Reproducibility, provenance and its limits}
\label{sec:repro}

Random number generation is pinned per run coordinate: one helper derives the key from
(experiment, controller, seed) and seeds Python, NumPy and torch through a single
implementation, BLAS thread counts are fixed, and the 20 seeds are declared in the frozen
configuration rather than passed on the command line. Every result row carries a
\texttt{config\_hash} (\texttt{637c6b535a9e} for all waves reported here) and a
\texttt{git\_sha}. The hash is identical across the default- and priced-objective waves and
so cannot by itself discriminate between them, and the per-row variant fields only partly
can: \texttt{horizon} and \texttt{max\_solver\_failures} are written by the main runner but
by none of the supporting blocks, and \texttt{objective} only by waves generated after that
flag was added --- it is absent from the canonical main table, which is default-objective by
construction. The identifying key is therefore the results tree and block together with
these fields where present; \texttt{git\_sha} is \texttt{unknown} in a few early resumed
files.

A self-test runs the pipeline twice under identical seeds and compares SHA-256 digests of
the training states and actions, the ensemble coefficients, the STLSQ coefficients, the
neural-network weights and the closed-loop trajectory, together with the closed-loop $J$ to
twelve decimal places: all seven digests were identical on the pinned stack, and all nine
with the reinforcement-learning policies added. Its coverage is narrower than the study:
the closed-loop leg is a single surrogate-MPC rollout of one recipe on one season under the
default objective, and no supporting block, neither the identification ladder nor the
replay block, is exercised.

One caveat qualifies every reproducibility statement here, and it is a limit on what was
measured rather than a measurement. The self-test establishes bit-identity \emph{within}
one environment. Nothing in the results tree establishes it \emph{across} environments,
and no such claim is made: the study was executed in a single environment, so the
cross-environment comparison was never run and its magnitude is unknown.

The provenance record makes the gap explicit rather than hiding it: although
\texttt{repro.py} can emit an environment fingerprint (\texttt{--fingerprint}), the field

provenance map prints \texttt{env\_hash:\ n/a}. Waves are separated only by

\texttt{config\_hash} --- which identifies the code but not the machine it ran on.

What the tree does show is that closed-loop margins move at a magnitude the identifying
fields do not capture, even for a controller with no stochastic element. The hard-coded
heuristic returns a four-season mean of $-1.2061$~EUR/m$^2$ in the main harness and
$-1.2264$ in the tuning harness at identical \texttt{config\_hash}, the per-season
discrepancy growing monotonically over the four seasons ($0.003$, $0.013$, $0.027$,
$0.038$~EUR/m$^2$ for 2020--2023). A deterministic controller re-run on the same season
should return the same number; that it does not is chaotic amplification of last-bit
floating-point differences through a 5760-step nonlinear loop, which is the same
mechanism that makes agreement across operating environments implausible \emph{a priori}.
The rule adopted here is therefore that a whole wave is regenerated in one environment
and is never mixed cell-by-cell with another for closed-loop numbers. Because the
environment itself is not recorded in the manifests, this provenance scheme cannot support
a cross-platform guarantee; the reproducibility claim is within-environment only.

\section{Results}
\label{sec:results}

Throughout, the economic performance indicator (EPI) is the seasonal margin in
EUR\,m$^{-2}$, and constraint pressure is the seasonal count of simulation steps outside
the productive envelope. Result rows were deduplicated on
(\texttt{method}, \texttt{seed}, \texttt{test\_year}) before averaging, because several
waves were resumed after interruptions and the files overlap; runs in which the solver
exhausted its failure budget were excluded. All waves reported here carry the same
\texttt{config\_hash} (\texttt{637c6b535a9e}), which therefore identifies the code base but
\emph{not} the experimental variant; variants are identified by the per-row
\texttt{objective} and \texttt{horizon} fields and by the results tree. Two objectives
appear: the original stage cost, and the \emph{priced} stage cost, in which the actuator
weights were made consistent with the prices the economic criterion itself uses.
Section~\ref{sec:res-main} uses the priced objective for every controller that optimises a
surrogate stage cost, which is every model-predictive controller except the full-model
planner --- that one maximises the simulator's own economic return and has no weights to
reprice. The blocks generated by the supporting harness run under the original objective,
and this is stated wherever it matters. Finally, closed-loop trajectories
amplify platform-level floating-point differences over 5760 steps: the deterministic
rule-based controller evaluates to $-1.2061$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ in the main harness and
$-1.2264$ in the tuning harness under identical code and \texttt{config\_hash}, so waves
are compared within one environment and never cell-by-cell across environments.

\subsection{One-step and multi-step criteria rank the feature libraries oppositely}
\label{sec:res-ladder}

The identification ladder evaluated 72 labelled configurations (three feature libraries
$\times$ two polynomial degrees $\times$ four optimizers $\times$ three denoisers) at 20
seeds each, 1440 fits with no duplicates and a complete grid. Table~\ref{tab:ladder}
gives the degree-1, undenoised block and Figure~\ref{fig:kappa} plots the two prediction
criteria and the closed-loop outcome for all three libraries.
Panels~\ref{fig:kappa}a,b use the two sparse estimators in that block ($n=40$ fits per
library): open symbols mark fits that fail the $0.05$ divergence gate and crosses show the
per-library median and IQR. Panel~\ref{fig:kappa}c holds degree, denoiser, ensemble
estimator and threshold fixed, changes only the feature library, and adds the 17-feature
probe that deletes the bilinear boiler term ($n=80$ runs per controller).

Adding physically motivated features monotonically inflates the regression condition
number, from $\kappa=8.21$ (raw) through $24.52$ (physics without cross terms) to $53.43$
(full physics), and monotonically degrades free-running prediction: the median 24-h
rollout RMSE of the inside-air temperature rises from $3.31$ to $7.11$ to $30.84\,^\circ$C,
and the fraction of diverging rollouts from $0.000$ to $0.011$ to $0.130$. The mean
rollout error of the \texttt{physics} library, $1356.7\,^\circ$C, is an artefact of
divergent realisations (upper rollout quartile $2360.1$); the median is the only defensible
summary. $\kappa$ depends on the library and the denoiser, not on degree or optimizer. The
relation is equally clean at fixed optimizer, which matters because the closed-loop
comparison below holds the optimizer fixed: under the ensemble estimator alone the median
rollout errors are $2.67$, $10.99$ and $24.27\,^\circ$C and the diverged fractions
$0.0000$, $0.0208$ and $0.0767$.

This is the whole of what conditioning is claimed to predict in this work. It orders the
three libraries on open-loop multi-step stability, monotonically, and the ordering
reproduces on a held-out year (below). It does \emph{not} transfer to the closed loop. The
same three libraries, compared one factor at a time --- ensemble estimator, threshold
$0.05$, only the library changed --- return $+4.32$, $+0.28$ and $+2.75$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ over
four test seasons (Table~\ref{tab:main}), so the worst-conditioned library outscores the
middle one by a factor of ten and the series in $\kappa$ is non-monotone in closed loop.
Nothing in this subsection is therefore an explanation of the economic result: the
mechanism that does account for it is actuator-pathway survival, developed in
Section~\ref{sec:res-mech}.

The reversal against one-step accuracy is clean only for the two sparse estimators
(STLSQ and ensemble, lower panel of Table~\ref{tab:ladder}): there the one-step ordering
is strictly $\text{physics} < \text{physics\_no\_cross} < \text{raw}$
($1.675 < 1.728 < 1.862\,^\circ$C), the exact inverse of every multi-step ordering.
Pooled over all four optimizer labels the one-step ordering is not strict --- the raw
library also beats \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} on one-step error ($2.137$ vs.\ $2.174$).
The supportable statement is therefore: \emph{the library with the best one-step accuracy
is the worst on every multi-step metric}, and the strict inversion holds under sparse
estimation. Two properties of the grid qualify the counts: the \texttt{constrained} and
\texttt{sr3} labels are bit-identical in all 360 seed-cells, so 72 labels cover 54 distinct
fits and the pooled upper panel of Table~\ref{tab:ladder} double-weights that estimator;
and embeddability coincides exactly with the degree-1 filter here (36 of 36 against
0 of 36), so it is not an independent screen. Of the 72 labels, 32 pass both
pre-registered gates (embeddable and diverged fraction $\le 0.05$).

The pre-specified frozen recipe
(\texttt{physics\_no\_cross}/degree~1/ensemble/no denoising) passes both gates
(diverged fraction $0.0208$), but it ranks 8th of 72 on one-step error and 59th--60th on
every multi-step metric. The comparator \texttt{raw}/degree~1/STLSQ/no denoising
dominates it simultaneously on all three pre-registered axes: rollout RMSE $2.684$ vs.\
$10.496\,^\circ$C, diverged fraction $0.0000$ vs.\ $0.0208$, active terms $19.9$ vs.\
$28.25$. The frozen recipe is better on exactly one metric, one-step accuracy, which was
never a gate. The full \texttt{physics} library at the same degree and estimator is worse
still on the pre-registered axes --- 63rd of 72 on median rollout error, 64th on divergence
--- and it is one of the 40 labels that \emph{fail} the divergence gate
($0.0767 > 0.05$), so the open-loop procedure would have rejected it outright.

That last fact bounds what the selection rule may be claimed to do, and the bound belongs
here rather than in the discussion. The rule identifies the winner: \texttt{raw} is first
on multi-step stability and first in closed loop. It does not order the two losers. In
closed loop the library the gate rejects scores $+2.75$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ and the library the
gate passes scores $+0.28$ (Table~\ref{tab:main}) --- the reverse of their open-loop
ranking. The supportable claim is that multi-step stability is a better selection criterion
than one-step accuracy and picks the best of the three libraries, not that it ranks
libraries.

A held-out check (240 fits, 10 seeds $\times$ 3 libraries $\times$ 2 sparse optimizers
$\times$ 4 fit$\to$evaluate directions over 2018--2019) reproduces the library ordering in
both regimes, with held-out/in-sample median ratios of $\times 1.04$ (raw), $\times 1.27$
(physics\_no\_cross) and $\times 0.99$ (physics). It does not, however, isolate held-out
status as the operative factor: pooled over fit year, median rollout error on 2018 versus
2019 data is $2.80/2.61$ for raw, $16.94/2.70$ for physics\_no\_cross and $27.19/15.85$ for
physics, and physics\_no\_cross narrowly beats raw in the 2018$\to$2019 direction. The
honest reading is that the raw library is stable irrespective of evaluation year whereas
the physics libraries are strongly year-dependent.

\subsection{Tuning the rule-based reference}
\label{sec:res-tune}

The rule-based controller was tuned under the same 16-trial budget the
reinforcement-learning agents are recorded as having received --- 16 configurations drawn
i.i.d.\ from a fixed generator, evaluated alongside the hard-coded configuration entered as
trial 0, so 17 configurations were scored in all --- and selected on the training
seasons 2018--2019 only, the test seasons taking no part in selection.
Table~\ref{tab:tune} reports the outcome. The predictive controllers received no
hyper-parameter search of any kind (Section~\ref{sec:controllers}), so the budget is
matched to the reinforcement-learning family alone.

Tuning is worth $+3.49$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ over four test seasons ($-1.23 \to +2.26$), and the
gain is concentrated in the two adverse seasons, 2021 ($+4.99$) and 2023 ($+5.79$). The
selected solution is agronomically interpretable --- a wider diurnal swing (day setpoint
$22.9$ instead of $19.5\,^\circ$C, night $14.4$ instead of $16.5\,^\circ$C) with a lower
supplementary-lighting threshold --- and it is bought with constraint pressure
($2813 \to 4339$ violation steps per season). Two facts qualify the exercise. Only 2 of
the 16 random draws beat the hard-coded setpoints on the training score, placing the stock
configuration at roughly the 88th percentile of the search space: it was not arbitrary,
merely unsearched. And the heuristic is deterministic, giving $n=4$ with no run-to-run
dispersion; the spread across seasons is not an error bar.

\subsection{Simulation-based four-season economic comparison}
\label{sec:res-main}

Fifteen controllers were compared on the test seasons 2020--2023 (Table~\ref{tab:main};
Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}). Every \emph{surrogate-based} model-predictive controller ran
under the priced objective at horizon 20. The reinforcement-learning controllers and the
heuristic have no surrogate stage cost, and the full-model planner optimises the
simulator's own economic return directly rather than a weighted surrogate cost, so none of
the three was repriced and all are quoted from the canonical wave. Two of the fifteen ---
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_phys\_ens} and \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_phys} --- carry the full
\texttt{physics} library at the frozen recipe's threshold of $0.05$ and complete the
one-factor library series; they were run under the priced objective only, and they are the
only two rows of the table with no counterpart under the original stage cost.

The raw-library SINDy-MPC leads with an ensemble optimizer ($+4.32$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$) and with
STLSQ ($+3.83$), ahead of the two low-threshold \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} variants
($+3.09$, $+3.08$), the two full-\texttt{physics} variants ($+2.75$, $+2.48$), the tuned
heuristic ($+2.26$), the on-policy re-identified variants ($+1.66$, $+1.37$),
PPO~\cite{schulman2017} ($+0.45$), the frozen recipe ($+0.28$), the stock heuristic
($-1.21$), the full-model planner ($-2.65$, three seasons only), the neural-surrogate MPC
($-3.82$, 10 seeds) and SAC~\cite{haarnoja2018} ($-4.15$). The ranking of the nine SINDy
controllers is therefore not ordered by feature library: the two full-\texttt{physics}
controllers sit between the low-threshold \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} variants and the
frozen \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} recipe, and \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_phys\_ens} outranks
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_conf} by four places on a one-factor change of library alone --- same
degree, same denoiser, same optimizer, same threshold.

Figure~\ref{fig:pareto} places one marker per controller on the plane
(EPI $\uparrow$, violation steps $\downarrow$), with the non-dominated controllers joined
by a step line. The advantage is not Pareto-dominant. On that plane the non-dominated set,
recomputed over all fifteen controllers, still has
exactly five members --- \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_raw\_ens} ($+4.32$, 4130),
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_lowthr} ($+3.09$, 3692), \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_dense} ($+3.08$, 3686),
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_dense\_dagger} ($+1.37$, 3615) and \texttt{ppo} ($+0.45$, 1330).
Neither new controller enters it: \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_raw\_ens} dominates
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_phys\_ens} ($+2.75$, 4193) and \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_phys} ($+2.48$,
4174) on \emph{both} axes, higher margin at fewer violation steps, so the full physics
library buys nothing that the raw library does not already provide. PPO attains roughly
one third of the constraint pressure of the leader and is the only controller on the front
that trades margin for safety at that rate. The tuned heuristic is \emph{off} the front
under the priced objective, dominated on both axes by \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_dense}. Front
membership of \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_dense\_dagger} is marginal: 7 of its 80 runs terminated
early, and over completed runs only its violation mean rises to 3657 against 3683 for
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_dense}, narrowing the gap from 71 to 26 steps.

Paired tests against the tuned reference (Table~\ref{tab:wilcoxon}, Holm correction over a
pre-declared family of 15 controllers) place four controllers
significantly above it, all of them SINDy-MPC: both raw-library variants ($\Delta$ median
$+2.85$ and $+2.83$, 75/80 and 68/80 wins) and both low-threshold
\texttt{physics\_no\_cross} variants ($+0.74$, $+0.69$). Three controllers are not
separable from the reference and must be described that way rather than as below it:
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_phys\_ens} ($\Delta$ mean $+0.49$, median $+0.25$, 41/80,
$p_{\text{Holm}}=0.176$), \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_phys} ($+0.21$, $+0.29$, 44/80, $0.334$) and
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_conf\_dagger} ($-0.60$, $-0.84$, 31/80, $0.334$). Every other
controller in Table~\ref{tab:wilcoxon} is significantly below. The fifteenth member of the
correction family, omitted from the table, is the stock heuristic re-evaluated inside the
tuning harness: at $n=4$ the signed-rank test cannot fall below $p=0.125$, so it is
non-significant ($\Delta$ mean $-3.49$, 0/4, $p_{\text{Holm}}=0.334$) even though its point
estimate matches the significant \texttt{rule\_based} row.
Because the tuned heuristic is deterministic with one run per season, its value is a
constant reused across seeds: this is a one-sample signed-rank test against a constant,
and the reference carries no sampling uncertainty. In a separate paired comparison between
the two raw variants the test is null ($\Delta$ median $-0.011$, $p=0.60$) --- the library,
not the optimizer, carries the effect. One caution on reading these levels:
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_dense} and
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_lowthr} are effectively one controller under the priced objective
(per-run mean $|\Delta|=0.026$, four-season means $3.0822$ vs.\ $3.0888$), yet a paired
test between them returns $p=0.012$. Significance here is not magnitude, and the Holm
family size is a reporting choice that shifts every corrected level.

The comparison of default and price-aligned stage costs is itself a result. Matched on
seed and season, the priced objective improves \emph{both} axes for all eight MPC
controllers for which both objectives were run, significantly in every case. The two
full-\texttt{physics} controllers are not among those eight and exist only under the priced
objective, so nothing here is claimed about their objective sensitivity. The magnitude differs
by an order of magnitude between the two libraries that were run twice: $+2.74$ and $+2.68$
EUR\,m$^{-2}$ for \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_dense} and \texttt{sindy\_mpc\_lowthr}, with
violations falling from $\sim$6005 to $\sim$3690, against $+0.25$ and $+0.21$ for the
raw-library variants --- a ratio of $10.9$ to $12.8$ depending on which pair is taken.
Consequently the gap between the raw library and the best
\texttt{physics\_no\_cross} controller narrows from $+3.66$ to $+1.23$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$.
The direction is retained, but the magnitude is highly sensitive to whether resource
weights match the evaluation prices. Against the full
\texttt{physics} library the priced gap is $+1.56$ ($4.3173$ against $2.7540$), measured
under one objective only and therefore not decomposable in the same way.

\subsection{Evaluation on four unseen weather seasons}
\label{sec:res-unseen}

Four weather seasons (2014--2017) that took no part in identification, gate evaluation,
selection or tuning were used as an external check (Table~\ref{tab:unseen}). These runs
use the \emph{default} stage cost; they are
therefore not comparable in level with Table~\ref{tab:main}, only in ordering. The lead
holds: the raw-library ensemble controller averages $+6.42$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$
($\text{SD}=0.95$, $n=32$) against $+2.38$ for the frozen recipe, $+1.07$ for the dense
\texttt{physics\_no\_cross} variant and $-0.33$ for the hard-coded heuristic, and it wins in 32 of 32

%% -----------------------------------------------------------------------------
\subsection{Mechanism}
\label{sec:res-mech}

What determines the closed-loop margin is not the conditioning of the regression but
whether the actuator pathway survives thresholding. Conditioning does predict one thing
cleanly, and only that: open-loop multi-step stability, monotonically in the library
(Section~\ref{sec:res-ladder}), as reported for ill-conditioned sparse regression
\cite{cortiella2021} and as expected from the identification-for-control literature
\cite{gevers1993,vandenhof1995}. It does not predict the economics. The three libraries
compared one factor at a time (Table~\ref{tab:survival3}) put the worst-conditioned basis
in the \emph{middle} of the economic ranking, ten times above the middle-conditioned one,
so the closed-loop series in $\kappa$ is non-monotone and no monotone conditioning
argument can be made from it. The quantity that does track the ranking is the fraction of
seeds in which the boiler-input coefficient $\xi_{u_{\mathrm{boil}}}$ is retained by the
sparsity threshold: the two libraries at $0.55$ survival score $+4.32$ and $+2.75$
EUR\,m$^{-2}$, and the one library at $0.15$ scores $+0.28$.

The comparison is one-factor by construction: degree, denoiser, optimizer and threshold
($0.05$) are held at the frozen recipe's values and only the feature library changes, over
four seasons and 20 seeds with zero truncated runs. The same pattern appears in the STLSQ
arm, where the raw library gives $+3.83$ (survival $0.50$) and the full physics library
$+2.48$ (survival $0.55$); that arm has no four-season \texttt{physics\_no\_cross}
controller at threshold $0.05$, but the $\lambda = 0.05$ level of the sparsity sweep is
exactly that configuration on season 2020, and it reproduces the anomaly --- survival
$0.10$ and $+4.04$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$, against $+8.40$ (raw) and $+5.82$ (physics) on the same
season. Reading across harnesses is what the environment caveat of the section preamble
warns about, so this is offered as a consistency check on the shape and not as a fourth
measurement.

Why the middle library is worse than either neighbour had one candidate explanation, and
it has now been tested directly and rejected. The three libraries are nested:
\texttt{raw} is the 11 measured signals; \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} adds saturation
vapour pressure, vapour-pressure deficit and screen-attenuated radiation, which are
nonlinear transforms of measured states and therefore near-collinear with them;
\texttt{physics} adds four bilinear state\,$\times$\,input terms on top, among them
$T_{\text{in}}u_{\mathrm{boil}}$. The candidate explanation held that the middle library
carries enough collinearity for the threshold to destabilise the \emph{direct} boiler
term, while the full library --- worse conditioned still --- keeps the bilinear term as a
detour able to carry the heating pathway once the direct one is cut. That account makes a
sharp, falsifiable prediction: deleting exactly the bilinear feature from the 18-feature
full library --- sixteen of eighteen features, and essentially all of the collinearity,
unchanged --- should collapse the truncated library onto the middle one, with boiler
survival near $0.15$ and margin near $+0.28$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$, not leave it near
$+2.75$ and $0.55$.

The prediction fails (Table~\ref{tab:survival3}, fourth row pair). The 17-feature library
(\texttt{physics\_no\_tuboil}), refit at both sparse estimators at threshold $0.05$ and
simulated over the same four seasons $\times$ 20 seeds with zero truncated runs, keeps the
full library's conditioning ($\kappa = 52.3$ against $53.4$, mean over 20 refits), its
open-loop instability (median rollout $24.2$~$^\circ$C, divergence $0.084$) and ---
decisively --- its closed-loop level: $+2.11$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ under the ensemble estimator
and $+2.28$ under STLSQ. That is significantly above the middle library (mean paired
difference $+1.83$, 62 of 80 pairs, $p_{\text{Holm}} = 4.4\times10^{-6}$ over the four
contrasts of this block) and at most slightly below the full library (median $-0.18$,
$p_{\text{Holm}} = 6.4\times10^{-3}$ under the ensemble estimator; the STLSQ arm is not
separable, $p = 0.089$). Boiler-term survival moves only part of the way down, to $0.40$
(8 of 20 replicates; exact McNemar test against the full library $p = 0.55$), and nowhere
near the predicted $0.15$. The small significant cost of the deletion under one estimator
suggests the bilinear term shares the threshold's pressure with the direct term rather
than substituting for it. With the detour ruled out, the margin ordering across the four
libraries continues to track survival --- $0.15 \rightarrow +0.28$, $0.40 \rightarrow
+2.11$, $0.55 \rightarrow +2.75$ and $+4.32$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ --- while \emph{which} feature
or correlation pattern makes the threshold sever the direct term in
\texttt{physics\_no\_cross} becomes an open question, stated as such in
Section~\ref{sec:discussion}.

The controlled evidence for the mechanism is the single-coefficient ablation, and it is
the only randomised element in this section. On the frozen recipe, paired over 20 seeds
and one season, the boiler coefficient is re-inserted from the same seed's low-threshold
fit with nothing else changed, lifting survival from $0.15$ to $1.00$. Under the priced
objective the effect is significant ($p = 5.9\times10^{-4}$, $p_{\text{Holm}}=
1.2\times10^{-3}$ over the two knock tests, positive in 16 of 20 replicates) but typically
small and heavily right-skewed: the median is $+0.21$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ and the mean $+1.92$,
nine times the median and carried by a few seeds. All three statements belong together;
the median is the defensible summary of the typical case and the mean is the defensible
summary of the aggregate, and quoting either alone misrepresents the distribution. Under
the default objective the same test gives a median of $+3.05$ at essentially the same
significance, so the direction survives price alignment while its typical magnitude falls
by a factor of about 14. Much of the apparent value of the coefficient is therefore
attributable to resource weighting rather than to the term alone. Knock-out is not significant under either
objective and its median is exactly zero, for the plain reason that the term is already
absent in 17 of the 20 baseline seeds: there is usually nothing to remove. Cross-term
interaction tests, run on the full \texttt{physics} library at threshold $10^{-6}$, are
uniformly non-significant and two of three point estimates flip sign between the
objectives, so that block supports no direction. In particular it does not test the
detour: at $10^{-6}$ the boiler term is retained in all 20 seeds, so the block removes
coefficients from a model that never lost any, which is the opposite of the situation the
detour is meant to explain.

Figure~\ref{fig:lambda} combines the within-library dose-response and the randomised
single-coefficient ablation. Panels~\ref{fig:lambda}a,b place margin and boiler-term
survival on the same sparsity-threshold grid; panel~\ref{fig:lambda}c shows the
seed-paired knock-in effect under each objective, with the median separated from the
right-skewed mean. Within one library the association is a dose-response. The sparsity sweep
(Table~\ref{tab:lambda} and Figure~\ref{fig:lambda}, \texttt{physics\_no\_cross}, season
2020 only, 20 seeds per level) shows the boiler term dying between $\lambda=0.03$
(survival $0.95$) and $\lambda=0.06$ (survival $0$) in both waves, whose survival profiles
differ in one cell only ($\lambda=0.04$: $0.35$ priced against $0.30$ original, one seed,
and a consequence of the execution-environment drift noted above rather than of the
objective). Levels at which at least one seed retains the term average $+5.77$
EUR\,m$^{-2}$ against $+3.47$ for levels at which none does, under the priced objective
($+3.46$ vs.\ $+2.77$ under the original). The separation is not monotone under either
objective --- the worst retaining level ($4.04$, priced) is below the best non-retaining one
($4.61$) --- so the statement ``EPI is monotone in boiler-term survival'' is false as
written, here as it is across libraries. The priced weights raise EPI at every one of the
13 levels ($+0.34$ to $+2.95$) and cut violations at every level.

Table~\ref{tab:twobytwo} decomposes EPI over the four test seasons by library and by
whether the boiler term survived, now over all three libraries. Inside
\texttt{physics\_no\_cross} the gap is $+2.34$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ and inside \texttt{raw}
$+1.13$; inside \texttt{physics} it is $-0.22$ and not significant. The raw library with
the term \emph{dropped} ($+3.48$) still beats every cell of both physics libraries,
including \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} with the term \emph{kept} ($+2.36$), so survival does
not exhaust the library effect either. This decomposition is observational, not a
randomised ablation --- survival is a property of the fit, constant across the four seasons
of a seed, so the effective sample is 20 seeds per controller and not 80 runs, and the
cells differ in composition as well as in the factor of interest: under the priced
objective the ``kept'' cell of \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} is filled by the three recipes
that never lose the term (80 runs each), plus most of one re-identified variant (68 of 80),
plus the 12 runs in which the frozen recipe happens to keep it; its ``dropped'' cell is
68/80 the frozen recipe. Between seeds the boiler
association is significant only inside \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} ($p=0.0015$ priced,
$p=0.0006$ original) and not inside \texttt{raw} ($p=0.136$, $p=0.316$) or
\texttt{physics} ($p=0.178$). The causal estimate remains the knock-in median of $+0.21$,
not the $-0.2$ to $+2.3$ spread the three observational splits suggest.

Four limits qualify the whole of this subsection. The randomised ablation is
single-season (2020) and single-recipe, so the causal estimate is not measured under the
seasonal variation that the rest of the results report. The knock and cross blocks are
anchored on different baselines (survival $0.15$ against $1.00$) and on different
libraries, so their deltas are not one ablation family. The library comparisons ---
including the term deletion, which is a structural edit and not an assigned factor ---
are randomised in the seed but not in the library: libraries are not assigned, they are
chosen, so these are controlled comparisons rather than experiments on the mechanism.
And survival is measured on one coefficient of one actuator; whether the same account
holds for the lamp and CO$_2$ pathways was not tested.

\subsection{Alternative explanations}
\label{sec:res-alt}

\textit{Model accuracy is not the binding constraint.} A planner using the simulator's own
dynamics ranks in the lower third ($-2.65$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$), and its shortfall cannot be
attributed to the price-aligned surrogate cost of Section~\ref{sec:res-main}: it never optimised
the weighted surrogate cost in the first place, but maximised the simulator's own economic
return over its horizon. What it does have is a weak optimiser --- a warm-started
cross-entropy search over a few dozen candidate sequences --- so the result is about
optimisation quality, not model quality. Its 2022 season is absent from
Table~\ref{tab:main} because all 20 runs hit the common solver-failure budget of 100, at
about $95\,\%$ of the season (season fraction $0.936$--$0.987$, mean $0.950$). This is a
budget artefact, not infeasibility: with the budget
raised to $10^5$ the season completes on both tested seeds, requiring 140 and 103 failures
and yielding $-1.12$ and $-0.56$ (mean $-0.84$). Imputing that mean gives a four-season
figure of about $-2.20$ instead of $-2.65$; both should be quoted, and the correct phrasing
is that the full model does not fit the common budget, not that it cannot be solved. Its
rank is unaffected either way.

\textit{Prediction error along the operating trajectory is small.} Replaying both
\texttt{physics\_no\_cross}
surrogates along the full-model planner's own closed-loop trajectory (5760 steps, 5 seeds,
season 2020) gives one-step temperature RMSE of $1.89\pm0.09$ and $1.82\pm0.04\,^\circ$C
($R^2 = 0.82$ and $0.83$) and 24-h rollout RMSE of $2.62$ and $2.49\,^\circ$C, with zero
diverging rollouts out of 100 at every horizon. The same frozen recipe measures about
$10.5\,^\circ$C on the excitation data of the ladder --- roughly four times larger. The
surrogate is far more accurate along a smooth closed-loop trajectory than on PRBS-excited
identification data, and the ladder number must not be read as its error in operation.

\textit{The horizon was never tuned, and correcting for it does not change the ordering.}
At horizon 8 every MPC improves over horizon 20 on matched seeds and seasons
(\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_raw\_ens} $+4.58$ vs.\ $+4.29$, $\Delta=+0.29$, 33/40;
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_lowthr} $+2.78$ vs.\ $+0.67$, $\Delta=+2.11$, 40/40;
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_dense} $+2.65$ vs.\ $+0.53$, $\Delta=+2.12$, 40/40;
\texttt{sindy\_mpc\_conf} $+0.90$ vs.\ $-0.40$, $\Delta=+1.30$, 37/40), with no rank
change. The size of the gain is inversely related to multi-step prediction quality --- $+0.29$
for the raw library against $+1.3$ to $+2.1$ for the \texttt{physics\_no\_cross} variants
--- which is exactly the compounding-error mechanism. The two full-\texttt{physics}
controllers were not in this block, so this argument is not extended to them. A single-controller horizon sweep confirms the direction
($6.11$, $5.13$, $3.63$, $0.91$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ at $h=8,12,20,30$, with dispersion growing
from $\text{SD}=0.88$ to $4.03$). The asymmetry runs against the MPCs: the learned
controllers received 16 tuning trials and the horizon was never tuned for any MPC.

\textit{The bootstrap draw of the ensemble optimizer is a real but secondary axis.}
Resampling the ensemble draw (6 seeds $\times$ 6 draws $\times$ 4 seasons, original
objective) leaves the raw-library controller leading in 32 of 36 seed$\times$draw cells
($0.89$). Its spread across draws within a seed averages $0.26$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ against
$1.11$ for the frozen recipe and $2.04$ for the re-identified variant, but that mean is
not representative: five of the six seeds lie at or below $0.25$ and one seed alone
contributes $1.18$. The controller has one bad basin rather than uniform insensitivity to
the draw. This wave disagrees with the canonical 20-seed, 10-draw wave on the frozen
recipe's boiler survival ($0.00$ vs.\ $0.12$) and on the leadership ordering, so the two
must be cited separately and never merged.

\subsection{Adaptation and safety}
\label{sec:res-safety}

Three adaptation modes were compared on the three harder seasons (2021--2023) with 20
seeds $\times$ 10 ensemble draws per condition, 600 runs each, under the original
objective. On-policy re-identification --- the surrogate is rolled out in closed loop, the
visited states are appended to the training set and the model is re-estimated, with no
expert queried, so this is not DAgger \cite{ross2011} --- gives $-0.39$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$
against $-1.58$ for the static model (paired $\Delta = +1.19$, median $+0.41$, 380/600,
$p=5.0\times10^{-16}$) and reduces violations from 5424 to 4654. Recursive coefficient
adaptation by extended Kalman filter is harmful: $-2.95$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$
($\Delta = -1.36$ against static, $p=7.4\times10^{-14}$) with 234 of 600 seasons
terminating early. All three are net-negative in absolute terms because 2020, the
favourable season, is absent from this block.

The Mahalanobis-distance safety guard \cite{mahalanobis1936,lee2018} does not pay. Guarded
runs average $-2.59$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ against $-1.91$ unguarded; the paired mean difference is
$-0.69$ but the paired median is $+0.19$ with the guard better in 335 of 600 pairs
($p=0.0047$), because the guard shortens a heavy left tail while shaving the centre; both
statistics must be reported. Violations are \emph{worse} under the guard ($+430$ steps,
worse in 529 of 600, $p=5\times10^{-85}$), and 179 of 600 guarded seasons terminate early;
restricted to completed seasons the margin difference is $-0.33$ and not significant
($p=0.081$). The underlying detector is weak: the area under the ROC curve is
$0.663\pm0.042$ for the distance statistic and $0.627\pm0.058$ for ensemble spread
\cite{lakshminarayanan2017}, barely above chance. The guard's activation count was never
recorded, so the natural explanatory variable is unmeasured. This is a negative result and
is reported as one.

The residual-based fault supervisor, in contrast, works (Table~\ref{tab:faults}). It
improves the margin under all six injected faults (paired over 20 seeds, all
$p\le1.7\times10^{-3}$, and unchanged in that bound under Holm correction over the six)
and collapses the
dispersion from $\text{SD}=1.16$--$5.48$ to $0.29$--$0.39$: it buys predictability more
than margin. Violations fall sharply (9131 to 5292 under a dead vent; 5198 to 2660 under a
stuck boiler). Two faults remain net-destructive even when supervised. One result deserves
plain statement rather than burial: with the lamps dead, the supervised fallback ($+6.33$)
outperforms the nominal fault-free controller ($+3.63$), which implies that the nominal MPC
over-uses supplementary lighting.

\subsection{Sensitivity to design parameters and to prices}
\label{sec:res-sens}

Figure~\ref{fig:perturb} separates two sensitivity questions: panel~\ref{fig:perturb}a
shows the run-level response to coefficient perturbation, whereas panel~\ref{fig:perturb}b
re-scores fixed trajectories over a fruit-price $\times$ energy-price grid. Perturbing the
identified coefficients degrades the closed loop non-uniformly
(Table~\ref{tab:sens}). Mean EPI falls from $+4.11$ at a
$2\,\%$ perturbation to $-8.13$ at
$20\,\%$, a span of $12.24$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$, while the median falls only from $+4.47$ to
$-0.31$, a span of $5.72$: degradation takes the form of a growing minority of
catastrophic runs, not a uniform decline. Only the two largest levels are significant
against the smallest on per-seed means ($p=0.0059$ at $15\,\%$, $p=0.0020$ at $20\,\%$;
$p>0.6$ below that), and the $2\,\%\to5\,\%$ step is already inverted. The canonical
coarser grid, which also covers $30\,\%$, gives $+1.67$, $-13.41$, $-4.77$ at
$10/20/30\,\%$ --- a span of $15.08$ and non-monotone; the finer rerun stops at $20\,\%$, so
that non-monotonicity is unreproduced rather than refuted. The coefficient-sensitivity
span is several times larger than the mean differences among the leading controllers in
Table~\ref{tab:main}. Varying the sparsity threshold of the same controller spans $5.00$
EUR\,m$^{-2}$ on this 10-seed rerun but only $1.87$ on the canonical 20-seed grid; the two
disagree by about $1$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$ even on matched seeds at the two lowest thresholds, so
the span of this factor is not well determined and only the direction should be relied on.
Violations rise by half between the extreme levels (6167 at threshold $0.01$ against 9570
at $0.20$) but \emph{not} monotonically: the $0.05$ level, at which the boiler term is lost
in 9 of 10 seeds, has the fewest violations of the four (5155), and the canonical grid
reproduces that dip (6263, 5423, 8440, 9219). Varying the horizon spans $4.76$--$5.21$.

Re-scoring the recorded physical quantities over a $3\times3$ price grid (fruit price
$\times$ energy scale, each spanning a factor of four) yields per-controller spans of
$31.5$ to $50.2$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$, median $38.3$. Prices therefore dominate design parameters
numerically, but the axes are not commensurable --- a factor of four in prices against
$\pm20\,\%$ on coefficients --- and no claim that ``prices matter more than design'' is
supportable from this comparison. What is supportable is that the ranking is not
price-invariant either: two distinct controllers win across the nine price points (the
heuristic at six, a re-identified SINDy variant at three). This last statement carries an
important restriction: the price grid was re-scored only for the ten controllers of the
canonical wave, and \emph{neither raw-library controller is in it}. The price sensitivity
of the leading controller of Table~\ref{tab:main} has therefore not been measured, and the
nine-point winner counts must not be read as a re-ranking of the main comparison.

An objective-scope audit established that the supporting harness for the design and
sensitivity blocks uses the default objective in every rollout call. The objective flag is
consumed only by the main and mechanism blocks; consequently, a directory label containing
``priced'' does not make these particular reruns price-aligned, and they are reported here
only as default-objective results. The mechanism sparsity sweep and the design threshold factor fit the same
model at the same levels, and under the original objective the two agree exactly on all 80
overlapping (level, seed) cells (max $|\Delta| = 0$). The rerun labelled priced, by
contrast, disagrees with the genuinely priced mechanism sweep on \emph{all} 40 overlapping
cells (mean $\Delta = +1.23$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$), while remaining within execution-environment
drift of the original-objective values (39 of 40 horizon cells and 36 of 40 threshold cells
within $0.2$ EUR\,m$^{-2}$; the remainder are chaotic amplifications). The coefficient and
threshold sensitivities have consequently never been measured under the priced objective.

\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

\subsection{Why physically motivated features can hurt, and why the damage is not
monotone in how many are added}
\label{sec:disc-mechanism}

The physically motivated regressors used here---saturation vapour pressure
$e_s(T_{\text{in}})$, vapour-pressure deficit $D$ and screen-attenuated
radiation $I(1-u_{\text{ThScr}})$---are algebraic functions of variables the raw
library already contains: no new excitation, only smooth near-collinear copies
of existing columns. Adding them, and then the four bilinear cross terms, raises
the condition number of the regression monotonically, and open-loop multi-step
stability degrades monotonically along the same direction. Over the ladder's
degree-one undenoised block (Section~\ref{sec:res-ladder}),
$\kappa = 8.2 \rightarrow 24.5 \rightarrow 53.4$, median 24~h rollout error
$3.31 \rightarrow 7.11 \rightarrow 30.84$~\textdegree C and divergence
$0.000 \rightarrow 0.011 \rightarrow 0.130$. One-step error does not order the
libraries along that direction at all: it is \emph{best} for the worst-conditioned
library ($2.02$~\textdegree C) while the middle library is marginally worse than
raw ($2.17$ against $2.14$), so what the ladder establishes is a contrast between
the extreme points, not a monotone reversal.
%% ladder_rerun/ladder_rerun.csv + ladder_rerun2.csv, degree == 1, denoise ==
%% 'none', dedup on (variant, degree, optimizer, denoise, seed); n = 80 fits per
%% library (4 optimizer labels x 20 seeds).  Recomputed 2026-08-14:
%% kappa 8.2070 / 24.5167 / 53.4254; rollout median 3.3123 / 7.1094 / 30.8435;
%% diverged 0.000 / 0.011 / 0.130; one-step 2.1374 / 2.1737 / 2.0216.
Table~\ref{tab:disc-libraries} shows the same contrast on the held-out
identification block, where the three libraries score $\kappa = 8.2$, $25.4$ and
$56.2$; that block is a different sample of fits (10 replicates per fit year
rather than 20 on both), so the two sets of figures must not be mixed within one
comparison.

The open-loop conditioning pattern does not extend monotonically to closed-loop
economics. Under the one-factor closed-loop comparison---ensemble optimiser,
threshold $0.05$, priced objective, four seasons
$\times$ 20 replicates, zero truncated runs, the feature library the only thing
that changes---the three libraries scored $+4.32$ (raw), $+0.28$ (physics without
cross terms) and $+2.75$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ (full physics). The series is
\textbf{non-monotone in $\kappa$}: the worst-conditioned library beats the middle
one by $+2.47$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ on average (median $+2.39$, 63 of 80 paired runs,
$p=1.3\times10^{-8}$), and it does so in each of the four seasons taken
separately.
%% Three-library one-factor comparison: phys_lib/main_physlib.csv +
%% priced_main/*.csv + priced_dagger/*.csv, dedup on (method, seed, test_year),
%% abort rule removes 0 rows, n = 80 per controller.  Recomputed 2026-08-14:
%% sindy_mpc_raw_ens +4.3173, sindy_mpc_conf +0.2818, sindy_mpc_phys_ens +2.7540.
%% Per season (raw / no-cross / full): 2020 9.10 / 3.88 / 6.35;
%% 2021 -1.56 / -3.16 / -1.91; 2022 4.62 / -0.31 / 3.07; 2023 5.10 / 0.72 / 3.51.
Conditioning therefore predicts open-loop multi-step stability cleanly but is not a
sufficient explanation for the non-monotone closed-loop economics.

What the closed-loop ordering does track is whether the boiler term survives
thresholding. The direct coefficient $\xi_{u\text{Boil}}$ is retained in 11 of 20
identification replicates with the raw library, 11 of 20 with the full physics
library, and 3 of 20 with the physics library without cross terms; the two
libraries at 11 of 20 score $+4.32$ and $+2.75$, and the one at 3 of 20 scores
$+0.28$.
%% Survival 0.55 / 0.15 / 0.55 from the same priced pool.  Survival is a property
%% of the fit and is constant across the four seasons of a replicate (verified:
%% 0 of 100 method-seed cells vary), so n = 20 replicates, not 80 runs.
Restated as a mechanism: what matters is not how well conditioned the regression
is, but whether the pathway through which an actuator reaches the controlled state
survives sparsification. A surrogate whose boiler column has been thresholded away
cannot be steered with the boiler, however accurate it is one step ahead and
however well conditioned the fit that produced it.

The controlled evidence for that reading is the single-coefficient ablation.
Reinstating the deleted
coefficient in the frozen recipe is the only randomised manipulation in the
mechanism block---library, scalers, every other term and the MPC construction held
fixed, one number changed. Under the priced objective the effect is significant and
typically small: positive in 16 of 20 replicates, $p=5.9\times10^{-4}$
($1.2\times10^{-3}$ after Holm correction over the two knock tests; $1.8\times10^{-3}$
if the family is widened to the four mechanism contrasts, as in
Figure~\ref{fig:lambda}), median $+0.21$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ with
interquartile range $[+0.02,\,+4.49]$, mean $+1.92$ (Figure~\ref{fig:lambda}c).
The mean is nine times the median because a minority of replicates carry it, so both
summaries are required. Under the default, non-price-aligned objective the median is
$+3.05$; the contrast shows that the magnitude, unlike the direction, depends strongly
on economic weighting.

%% Recomputed 2026-08-14: median 0.2134, mean 1.9165, IQR [0.024, 4.491],

%% gives 1.17e-3.  Under the original objective: median 3.052, mean 2.521,
%% 17/20, p = 3.2e-4.

Survival gates the outcome; it does not order it, and two measurements say so.
Among the two libraries that keep the term, the raw one still wins by
$+1.56$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ (median $+0.97$, 67 of 80, $p=2.5\times10^{-9}$)---and it is
also the one with the better rollout, so the open-loop criterion of
Section~\ref{sec:disc-rule} separates the two survivors where survival does not.
And in the decomposition over (library) $\times$ (term retained), the raw library
\emph{without} the term ($+3.48$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$) still beats the physics library
\emph{with} it ($+2.36$).
%% 2x2 cells: priced pool, controllers grouped by feature_variant per
%% regen/regen_config.py.  Survival is constant across the four seasons of a
%% seed, so the effective n is 19 and 20 seeds, not 76 and 320 runs.
That split is observational---survival is a property of each fit, not a randomised
factor, and it is confounded with recipe---so the conservative reading is that
survival marks which fits collapse rather than measuring the whole library effect.

One plausible explanation for why the middle library is worse than \emph{both} of its
neighbours is that the library without cross terms carries enough collinearity for
the threshold to destabilise the direct boiler term, but---unlike the full
library---holds no bilinear $T_{\text{in}}u_{\text{Boil}}$ term able to carry the
heating pathway once the direct term is cut: the worse-conditioned library has a
detour, the middle one has neither stability nor a detour. Two
observational figures were consistent with it, and neither is significant: inside
the full physics library, replicates that lose the direct term score no worse than
those that keep it ($+3.10$ against $+2.48$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, 9 and 11 replicates,
$p=0.25$), whereas inside the library without cross terms losing it costs
$+2.26$ ($+2.20$ against $-0.06$, 3 and 17 replicates, $p=0.18$).

\subsection{A cheap open-loop rule for selecting a control-oriented surrogate}
\label{sec:disc-rule}

Two things follow for practice, and they are different in kind. The first is a
screening rule: score candidates open-loop on (i) free-running multi-step rollout
error at a horizon no shorter than the one the model will be used at and (ii) the
fraction of rollouts that diverge---both computed on the identification data in
seconds, before any closed-loop season is simulated---and keep one-step error out
of the gate. The condition number belongs in the report as a diagnostic but not in
the ranking: it predicts (i) and (ii) accurately and closed-loop margin not at all
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-mechanism}). Ours scored 96 steps (24~h) against a 20-step
(5~h) control horizon---a stress test rather than a matched one, so the screening
horizon has to be reported with the score. In our ladder the
raw configuration dominates the frozen physics-informed recipe on all three
pre-registered axes at once (Section~\ref{sec:res-ladder}), and the two
controllers built on that library then take the top two places in closed loop.
The frozen recipe leads on exactly one metric, one-step accuracy (rank 8 of 72
against rank 10), which was not a gate. The screen is close to free: the whole
ladder cell costs a few seconds per fit, against a simulated season per candidate
for a closed-loop evaluation.

The strength of that claim must be stated at the resolution the three libraries
allow. Over the series, the screen \emph{identifies the closed-loop winner} and
one-step accuracy ranks that same winner \emph{last}; but neither open-loop
criterion reproduces the full closed-loop ordering, because rollout error places
full physics third where the closed loop places it second. What is established is
the choice of the top candidate and the rejection of one-step accuracy as a
selection criterion---not a rank correlation across the series.

%% < 24.27 (physics).  Closed loop (priced pool): raw +4.32 > physics +2.75 >
%% no-cross +0.28.  So rollout and EPI agree on rank 1 and disagree on 2 and 3.

The second thing that follows is not a ranking but a check, and it is the one the
new mechanism makes necessary. After sparsification, read the fitted coefficient
matrix and confirm that every actuator the controller is allowed to move still has
a surviving path to the states it is meant to move. The check costs nothing---it
inspects numbers already computed---and it is the variable the closed loop turned
out to be sensitive to, whereas a well-conditioned regression is not a guarantee
that the check passes and an ill-conditioned one is not a guarantee that it fails.
When the check does fail, the available responses are a lower threshold, a
different basis, or a constraint protecting the actuator columns; we tested only
the first, by hand, on one coefficient, and it bought back a median of
$+0.21$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$.

Two qualifications. The ladder's rollout value is a \emph{comparator}, not a
specification: along the oracle controller's own closed-loop trajectory the same
frozen recipe predicts indoor temperature to $2.62$~\textdegree C at 24~h, four
times better than the $10.50$~\textdegree C it scores on PRBS-excited
identification data.
%% regen/results/v3_parity/parity_v3.csv (120 rows, 5 seeds, season 2020).
The held-out block supports the ordering but licenses a different claim: the
ordering is identical in-sample and held-out, yet the dominant factor is the
\emph{evaluation} year. The physics library without cross terms scores a median
rollout error of $16.9$~\textdegree C evaluated on 2018
and $2.7$~\textdegree C on 2019 whatever year it was fitted on, and in one of
the four fit$\rightarrow$evaluate directions it narrowly beats the raw library
($2.687$ against $2.722$). What is defensible is that the raw library is stable
regardless of evaluation year while the physics libraries are year-dependent.

The third piece of guidance is therefore negative: do not treat a basis function's
physical motivation as evidence that it belongs in the library. Every feature added
here is an algebraic function of columns already present, and the two groups in
which they were added both cost multi-step stability---the three non-cross features
take rollout error from $3.31$ to $7.11$~\textdegree C, the four cross terms take
it on to $30.84$---while neither group buys back a closed-loop margin above the raw
library's. Nor does the \emph{count} of added features order the damage: adding
three is worse in closed loop than adding seven
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-mechanism}), so a practitioner cannot reason from ``I added
only a few, physically sensible terms'' to ``the cost is small''. What we cannot
say is which individual feature is responsible for anything: they entered in two
groups of three and four, no feature was added or removed alone, and the one
single-feature deletion that would matter most is the untested experiment named
above. Motivation earns a candidate a place in the ladder, and nothing more.

\subsection{Sparsity is exonerated}
\label{sec:disc-sparsity}

The winner is the sparser model on both counts---11 candidate features against
14, and $19.9$ retained terms against $28.25$---so sparsity cannot be what the
physics-informed variants lost. Nor is more sparsity better within a library:
over the sparsity sweep of Table~\ref{tab:lambda} margin falls as terms are
removed, and violation steps rise with it, from about $3850$ to $9090$ between
the endpoints. The decline is not monotone across the thirteen levels, and
the between-replicate SD reaches $4.14$, larger than several of the steps, so
only the endpoints carry the comparison. The three-library series settles the
question in the other direction as well: across the ensemble triple, in which the
library is the only thing that changes, retained terms run
$20.2 \rightarrow 28.25 \rightarrow 37.2$ while closed-loop margin runs
$+4.32 \rightarrow +0.28 \rightarrow +2.75$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$. The least sparse model
of the three is not the worst; the middle one is.

Term count orders performance in neither direction; the basis does. The two

library rather than in the estimator. This is a failure to reject, not a
demonstration of equivalence: the mean gap is not negligible, only too disperse
to establish at $n=80$.

\subsection{Relation to prior work}
\label{sec:disc-related}

The finding instantiates the identification-for-control programme---model
quality cannot be judged independently of the control task the model
serves~\cite{gevers1993,vandenhof1995,ljung1999,hjalmarsson2005,controlorientedsurvey2025}---and
the objective-mismatch literature of model-based reinforcement learning, where
prediction likelihood is a poor proxy for control
performance~\cite{lambert2020,wei2024,farahmand2017,jackson2022}. Our
contribution there is constructive rather than diagnostic: an open-loop screen
costing seconds per candidate identifies the closed-loop winner, whereas the
standard screen ranks that winner \emph{last}. The screen needs only free-running
rollouts, so it transfers in principle to any model class; but we exercised it on
sparse-regression libraries, and over the three of them it selects the winner
without reproducing the ordering of the two losers
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-rule}), so the correspondence is established as a choice of
top candidate, not as a rank correlation and not across model classes.
Section~\ref{sec:disc-defects} adds a second, less-studied mismatch: stage-cost
weights against the prices in the criterion, inside an otherwise correctly
specified problem. Collinearity in the library is what reweighted and
balance-guided sparse regression is designed to
mitigate~\cite{cortiella2021,balanceguided2026,belsley1980}, and our open-loop
measurements agree with that literature exactly: $\kappa$ orders multi-step
stability without exception. What we do \emph{not} find is the further step from
conditioning to closed-loop economics, where the ordering breaks
(Section~\ref{sec:disc-mechanism}). The consequence is not that library design
matters less but that it has to be scored on what the controller actually uses---a
point on which library optimisation scored by recursive long-term
prediction~\cite{yonezawa2026} converges from the opposite direction. Bagged
ensembling~\cite{fasel2022} changed neither outcome: it is statistically
indistinguishable from plain thresholded least squares on the raw library
($p=0.60$) and on the full physics library ($p=0.057$).
%% raw_ens - raw: mean +0.484, median -0.011, 33/80, p = 0.601.
%% phys_ens - phys: mean +0.278, median +0.111, 49/80, p = 0.057.
%% Paired Wilcoxon over (seed, test_year), priced pool.  Recomputed 2026-08-14.
%% Both are failures to reject, not demonstrations of equivalence.
Greenhouse comparisons of MPC and reinforcement learning have generally fixed the model
and varied the controller~\cite{morcego2023,mallick2025,vanlaatum2025,lin2021}; our
results show that the surrogate library can be comparably consequential.

The study also belongs to a developing simulation-based literature in
\textit{Agronomy}. Xu et al.\ used a mechanistic greenhouse--crop model to compare
open- and closed-loop optimal control and reported simulated yield and profit
\cite{xu2023agronomy}; Wen et al.\ combined an experimentally fitted photosynthesis
surrogate with multi-objective regulation of light and CO$_2$
\cite{wen2022agronomy}; and E{\'c}im-{\DJ}uri{\'c} et al.\ coupled transient building
simulation with an ANN after comparing modelled and measured greenhouse temperatures
\cite{ecimduric2024}. The present contribution differs by holding the mechanistic
greenhouse fixed and treating surrogate selection itself as the experimental factor. It
also provides a lower level of empirical evidence than studies that operate controllers
on a crop, such as the eight-week reinforcement-learning irrigation evaluation of
Padilla-Nates et al.\ \cite{padillanates2025}. Accordingly, our results support model and
controller design choices for subsequent testing; they do not establish agronomic efficacy
in a physical greenhouse.

\subsection{Limitations}
\label{sec:disc-limitations}

\begin{itemize}

\item \textbf{There is no physical-greenhouse validation.} Every climate trajectory,
crop response, resource use and margin is generated by GreenLight under ERA5-derived
weather. The published evaluation of GreenLight supports its use as a common testbed, but
does not validate the controllers developed here. The comparison excludes unmodelled
sensor bias, actuator wear and delay, crop heterogeneity, pests and disease, and operational
intervention by growers. Absolute EUR\,m$^{-2}$ values should therefore be read as outputs
of the virtual experiment; only comparative behaviour within the stated model and price
assumptions is established. Hardware-in-the-loop testing followed by a greenhouse pilot
with independently measured climate, energy use and marketable yield is required before
deployment.

\item \textbf{The advantage is not Pareto-dominant.} Five controllers hold the
front; PPO incurs $1330$ violation steps against the raw library's $4130$, at
$3.86$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ less margin. Superiority is conditional on how a grower
weights margin against corridor excursions.

\item \textbf{The MPC objective is necessarily a proxy.} The surrogate carries
no crop state, so revenue cannot be predicted and terminal biomass cannot be
priced---structural to the model class, and it blocks the natural terminal-cost
formulation.

\item \textbf{The tuned-heuristic comparison is not a paired test.} The
heuristic is deterministic and ran once per season, so its value is a constant
reused across replicates and carries no sampling uncertainty: the $+2.06$ above
is a one-sample signed-rank against that constant. Genuinely paired it collapses
to one replicate's four seasons ($+2.55$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, $n=4$), where the
minimum attainable $p$ is $0.125$ and nothing can be significant.
%% +2.06 = mean over all 80 raw_ens runs minus the per-season constant;
%% +2.55 = the same contrast restricted to seed 0, the only seed the heuristic
%% was run at (regen/results/n2_tune/tune_rb_n2.csv).

\item \textbf{Significance is not magnitude.} Two physics-informed variants
differ by $0.0066$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ over four seasons yet separate at $p=0.012$, so
listing both as front points is an artefact. Effect sizes accompany every
$p$-value, and Holm corrections are over a declared family of 15
controllers---another declaration moves them. This is not hypothetical: adding the
two full-\texttt{physics} controllers enlarged the family from 13 to 15 and moved
every corrected level in the main comparison, the raw-versus-physics contrast from
$7.7\times10^{-4}$ to $4.6\times10^{-4}$ among them, without any controller
crossing $0.05$.

\item \textbf{Coverage is uneven.} The neural-network MPC has 10 replicates and
40 runs against 20 and 80 elsewhere, 14 of them terminating early. The oracle
has no 2022 season---all 20 runs exhausted the solver-failure budget---but that
is an artefact: at a thousandfold budget the season completes at $-1.12$ and
$-0.56$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, so the reported $-2.65$ is a three-season mean and the
imputed four-season value is $\approx-2.20$. One front member owes part of its
position to truncation: over complete runs its violation margin narrows from 71
to 26 steps.

\item \textbf{The mechanism rests on a three-point library comparison, which is
few.} A claim about what does and does not order closed-loop performance is being
carried by three libraries, and the third point exists on one optimiser only: the
ensemble triple is complete, whereas at threshold $0.05$ under sequential
thresholded least squares only the raw and full-physics libraries were run
($+3.83$ and $+2.48$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$), so the raw-over-physics direction replicates
but the non-monotonicity itself does not exist as a three-point series on that
axis. Boiler-term survival is \emph{measured, not manipulated}, at library level:
the libraries differ in the basis, and survival is a downstream property of the
resulting fits. The only randomised manipulation anywhere in this argument is the
single-coefficient ablation, whose median effect is $+0.21$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$---so the
mechanism is supported by one controlled contrast of modest size plus a library
pattern that is measured rather than manipulated. A fourth library has already been
added---the term-deletion probe below---and did not disturb the pattern, but the
pattern remains observational at library level.

\item \textbf{What sets boiler-term survival is unresolved.} The candidate
structural explanation---that the bilinear $T_{\text{in}}u_{\text{Boil}}$ term is
what rescues the full physics library---has been tested by deleting exactly that
feature, same optimiser and threshold, four seasons $\times$ 20 replicates in
closed loop, and is rejected (Section~\ref{sec:disc-mechanism}): the 17-feature
library keeps the full library's conditioning, open-loop instability and
closed-loop level, and its survival ($0.40$) comes nowhere near the middle
library's $0.15$. The two observational figures once offered for the detour were
non-significant at 9--11 and 3--17 replicates and now stand only as absence of
contradiction. The non-monotonicity is an established fact without an established
cause.

\item \textbf{The mechanism block is single-season.} All 800 mechanism runs are
for 2020, so neither the $\lambda$ sweep nor the ablation generalises across
seasons. The knock-in and cross-term blocks rest on different baselines
(boiler-term survival $0.15$ against $1.00$) and are not mutually comparable;
the split by term survival is observational and confounded with recipe, and only
the knock-in contrast is controlled. The library comparison, by contrast,
covers all four test seasons, including the term-deletion probe.

\item \textbf{The held-out check rests on two training years.} With 2018 and
2019 alone, year-dependence cannot be separated from chance, and the evaluation
year dominates (Section~\ref{sec:disc-rule}).

\item \textbf{The design and sensitivity blocks use the default objective.} The support
harness hard-codes the default stage cost, so those sensitivities do not test the
price-aligned formulation. Under the default objective, perturbing identified coefficients
by $\pm15\,\%$ and $\pm20\,\%$
degrades margin significantly (9 of 10 and 10 of 10 replicates worse, span of
means $12.2$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$), but as a growing minority of catastrophic runs, not
a uniform decline---the median at $\pm20\,\%$ is only $-0.31$. This finer grid
stops at $\pm20\,\%$, so the non-monotonicity the coarser canonical grid shows
at $\pm30\,\%$ is unreproduced rather than refuted.

\item \textbf{The horizon was never tuned for any MPC.} Every MPC improves at
horizon 8 over horizon 20, by $+0.29$ for the raw library and
$+1.3\ldots+2.1$ for the physics-informed variants---consistent with rollout error
compounding inside the prediction horizon, which is the open-loop half of the
argument and is unaffected by Section~\ref{sec:disc-mechanism}; and an asymmetry
running against the MPCs, which
received no hyperparameter search while the reinforcement learning agents and
the heuristic received 16 trials each.

\item \textbf{Reproducibility is within-environment only, and cross-environment
reproducibility was never measured.} The pipeline reproduces nine digests
bit-for-bit on one machine. Whether closed-loop margins survive a change of
machine is \emph{unknown}: every wave reported here ran in one environment, and
the manifests do not even record which one (Section~\ref{sec:repro}). What is
measurable in the tree is that closed-loop margins move under changes far
smaller than a change of platform---the two harnesses score the
\emph{deterministic} heuristic differently in all four seasons at identical
configuration hash, by up to $0.038$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ and monotonically more with
each season. A controller with no stochastic element reproducing itself only to
two decimal places is the conservative reading of this limitation, and it is
enough to warrant the rule adopted: whole waves are regenerated in one
environment and never mixed cell-by-cell.

%% (+4.6304, -6.9846, +1.3009, -3.8524); per-season |delta| 0.0031, 0.0134,

The configuration hash does not discriminate between objective variants either,
so the results tree and block, not the hash, are the identifying key.

\item \textbf{Negative results stand as such.} The out-of-distribution guard
does not pay for itself, and the sign of its effect depends on which statistic is
taken --- mean against median, all pairs against completed seasons
(Section~\ref{sec:res-safety}). It truncates a left tail rather than raising the
centre, costs violations, ends $30\,\%$ of its seasons early, and rests on a
detector barely better than chance; its activation count was never recorded, so
the natural explanatory variable is missing. The extended Kalman filter observer
is worse than doing nothing ($-2.95$ against $-1.58$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$), $39\,\%$ of
its seasons terminating early.

\item \textbf{Additional scope limits.} One location, one planting date, one crop model
and one weather source are represented. The ranking is not price-invariant: two different
controllers win across a nine-point grid of fruit and energy prices---though

\section{Conclusions}
\label{sec:conclusions}

Within a GreenLight-based in silico greenhouse testbed, we asked which open-loop
criterion should select a data-driven surrogate for economic MPC and which model
property best explains the closed-loop result. Multi-step stability selects the
leading candidate, while survival of the actuator term through the sparsity
threshold---rather than conditioning alone---best tracks the non-monotone economic
outcome (Figure~\ref{fig:graphical-abstract}).

\begin{enumerate}

%% --- Finding 1 ---------------------------------------------------------
%% Source: regen/results/ladder_rerun/ladder_rerun.csv + ladder_rerun2.csv
%% (1440 rows = 20 seeds x 72 configs, zero duplicates).  QUOTED SLICE:
%%   degree == 1 AND denoise == 'none' AND optimizer in {stlsq, ensemble},
%%   n = 40 rows per library.  Recomputed 2026-08-14, raw / no_cross / physics:
%%   one-step 1.8616 / 1.7281 / 1.6753; median 24 h rollout 2.6683 / 10.5755 /
%%   24.2678; divergence 0.0000 / 0.0204 / 0.0758; non-zero 20.05 / 28.18 /
%%   36.98; kappa 8.207 / 24.517 / 53.425.
%% SCOPE WARNING, verified by recomputation: pooled over ALL 72 labels the
%%   one-step means are raw 2.703 < physics_no_cross 2.975 < physics 3.454, i.e.
%%   the raw library is best on one-step error too and there is NO reversal.  The
%%   reversal exists only in the degree-1 block (all denoisers) and is sharpest
%%   undenoised.  Do not restate it as a property of the whole ladder.
%% Held-out replication: regen/results/holdout/holdout_holdout.csv, in_sample
%%   == False, n = 40 per library: one-step 1.895 / 1.819 / 1.765, median
%%   rollout 2.722 / 15.421 / 21.568.
\item \textbf{One-step accuracy and multi-step stability rank the candidate
models in opposite orders.} In the first-order, undenoised block of a
72-configuration ladder, under two sparse estimators, physically motivated
features lowered one-step RMSE from $1.86$ to $1.68$~\textdegree C but raised
the median 24~h rollout RMSE from $2.67$ to $24.27$~\textdegree C. The reversal
is confined to that block --- pooled over all 72 labels the raw library predicts
better one step ahead too --- but it reproduces on a held-out year
\cite{lambert2020,wei2024,gevers1993,vandenhof1995,yonezawa2026}.

%% --- Finding 2 ---------------------------------------------------------
%% Four test seasons (2020-2023), priced objective: priced_main/*.csv,
%% priced_dagger/*.csv, phys_lib/main_physlib.csv, n2_tune/tune_rb_n2.csv,
%% final/main.csv.  Unseen seasons (2014-2017), DEFAULT objective:
%% n5_years/main_n5.csv.
%% "Fifteen controllers" = nine SINDy-MPC variants (raw, raw_ens, conf, dense,
%% lowthr, phys, phys_ens and two on-policy re-identification variants), nn_mpc,
%% ppo, sac, oracle_mpc and the rule-based heuristic in its stock and tuned form,
%% which are distinct controllers with distinct setpoints.  Section 3.4 still
%% says thirteen and must be updated with this section.
%% Replicates: 20 seeds for all except nn_mpc (10 seeds, n = 40), either form of
%% the heuristic (deterministic, one run per season, n = 4) and oracle_mpc
%% (n = 60; all 20 runs of 2022 dropped by the solver-abort rule).
%% Holm recomputed over the family of 15 (14 comparisons against raw_ens):
%%   lowthr raw p = 1.832e-4 -> Holm 4.625e-4; dense raw p = 1.542e-4 -> Holm
%%   4.625e-4 (monotonicity binds the pair); tuned heuristic raw p = 6.95e-12 ->
%%   Holm 6.26e-11, mean delta +2.056, better in 75 of 80 runs.
%% The test against either heuristic is a ONE-SAMPLE signed-rank test against a
%% constant -- see the footnote to Table~\ref{tab:headline}.
%% n5_years/main_n5.csv contains FOUR methods only (raw_ens, conf, dense,
%% rule_based); no RL, no NN-MPC, no planner, no tuned heuristic.
\item \textbf{The library selected by multi-step stability also gives the best
simulated closed-loop economics under the benchmarking safeguards.} Across
fifteen controllers on four test seasons the raw-library ensemble surrogate
\cite{fasel2022} led with $+4.32$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, against $+3.09$ for the best
physics-informed variant (Holm-corrected $p=4.6\times10^{-4}$, family of 15) and
$+2.26$ for the agronomic heuristic, re-tuned under the reinforcement-learning
agents' own 16-trial budget ($\Delta=+2.06$, 75 of 80 runs against a
deterministic reference) \cite{wilcoxon1945,holm1979}. Tuning was worth $+3.49$,
and aligning the actuator weights with the evaluation prices cut the raw-minus-physics
gap from $+3.66$ to $+1.23$. The direction is retained, but its magnitude is sensitive
to baseline tuning and economic weighting.

%% --- Finding 3 ---------------------------------------------------------
%% ONE-FACTOR comparison: ensemble optimiser, threshold 0.05, degree 1, no
%% denoising; ONLY the feature library changes.  Recomputed 2026-08-14 from
%% priced_main/*.csv (sindy_mpc_raw_ens, sindy_mpc_conf) and
%% phys_lib/main_physlib.csv (sindy_mpc_phys_ens); 4 seasons x 20 seeds each,
%% n = 80 per library, zero truncated runs, priced objective:
%%   raw               kappa  8.21   rollout  2.67   EPI +4.3173   survival 0.55
%%   physics_no_cross  kappa 24.52   rollout 10.58   EPI +0.2818   survival 0.15
%%   physics           kappa 53.43   rollout 24.27   EPI +2.7540   survival 0.55
%% Same one-factor change under STLSQ at 0.05: raw +3.8333, physics +2.4762.
%% kappa and rollout are monotone; EPI is NOT.  This retracts the conditioning
%% mechanism and the monotone-in-kappa claim outright.  Do not restore them.
%% Paired against raw_ens on (seed, test_year): conf median +2.873, 75/80,
%% p = 3.0e-14; phys_ens median +0.971, 67/80, p = 2.5e-9.
\item \textbf{Physically motivated features can reduce performance non-monotonically,
and conditioning does not order the economics.} Changing the feature library alone
--- same estimator, threshold and seeds --- the condition number rises
monotonically, $8.2 \rightarrow 24.5 \rightarrow 53.4$, the fits grow
\emph{denser} rather than sparser ($20.1 \rightarrow 37.0$ terms), and open-loop
rollout error rises with them, $2.67 \rightarrow 10.58 \rightarrow
24.27$~\textdegree C. Closed-loop margin does not: $+4.32 \rightarrow +0.28
\rightarrow +2.75$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, the worst-conditioned library beating the
intermediate one almost tenfold, with the same shape under the other estimator.
Conditioning predicts open-loop stability cleanly but does not order closed-loop
economics \cite{cortiella2021,balanceguided2026,belsley1980}.

%% --- Finding 4 ---------------------------------------------------------
%% Survival = fraction of identification replicates retaining a non-zero direct
%% uBoil -> t_in coefficient (run_regen._uboil).  Recomputed over the same
%% n = 80 rows per controller: raw_ens 0.55, conf 0.15, phys_ens 0.55.  Survival
%% is a property of the FIT, so the effective n is 20 seeds, not 80 runs.
%% Knock-in: priced_mech/mechanism_pricedMech*.csv, block == 'knock',
%%   test_year == 2020, deduped on (block, condition, seed, test_year), n = 20
%%   paired: median +0.2134, mean +1.9165, better in 16 of 20, p = 5.86e-4.
%% Within-library stratification, priced pool, Mann-Whitney:
%%   conf     kept n = 12 mean +2.199 vs cut n = 68 mean -0.057, p = 0.053 (n.s.)
%%   raw_ens  kept n = 44 mean +4.689 vs cut n = 36 mean +3.862, p = 0.44 (n.s.)
%%   phys_ens kept n = 44 mean +2.475 vs cut n = 36 mean +3.095, p = 0.48 (n.s.)
%%   The last two nulls were consistent with the detour reading; do not quote them
%%   as support, only as absence of contradiction.
%% The detour reading is FALSIFIED (2026-08-18): the 17-feature physics_no_tuboil
%%   library (regen/results/notuboil/) keeps kappa 52.3, EPI +2.11/+2.28 and
%%   survival 0.40 -- the predicted collapse onto physics_no_cross (~+0.3, ~0.15)
%%   did not occur.  The cross-term contrasts in priced_mech (block == 'cross',
%%   physics library at threshold 1e-6, n = 20 paired against that block's own
%%   baseline) are all non-significant: ko_uboil p = 0.33, ko_cross p = 0.48,
%%   ko_both p = 0.93 -- and they never could test severance, since baseline
%%   survival there is 1.00.  The bilinear term is named `t_uBoil' in the library
%%   (run_regen.CROSS_TERM).
\item \textbf{The closed-loop outcome follows whether the actuator pathway
survives thresholding.} The direct boiler coefficient survives in $55$, $15$ and
$55\,\%$ of fits of the three nested libraries --- the ordering of the margins, which
conditioning does not reproduce --- and in $40\,\%$ of fits of the 17-feature
deletion library, whose margin ($+2.11$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$) lands between, again in
survival order. Reinstating it in the recipe that loses it is
significant but small and right-skewed: median $+0.21$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, mean
$+1.92$, 16 of 20 replicates, $p=5.9\times10^{-4}$, one season. Stratifying that
recipe's own fits by survival agrees, not significantly ($p=0.053$). A candidate
bilinear temperature--boiler detour was tested by deleting that single feature and
is rejected; why the middle library
loses the term is open, that survival gates the outcome is not.

%% --- Finding 5 ---------------------------------------------------------
%% Pareto front recomputed 2026-08-14 over the family of 15 (priced_main/*.csv +
%% priced_dagger/*.csv + phys_lib/main_physlib.csv + final/main.csv +
%% n2_tune/tune_rb_n2.csv): raw_ens (+4.32, 4130), lowthr (+3.09, 3692),
%% dense (+3.08, 3686), dense_dagger (+1.37, 3615), ppo (+0.45, 1330).  Five
%% members, UNCHANGED by the two new controllers -- both are dominated.  Margin
%% gap raw_ens - ppo = 3.864.
%% Guard: final/guard.csv, paired on (seed, draw, test_year), n = 600 pairs:
%%   median +0.189, mean -0.686, violations +430, AUC 0.6625 against 0.6269 for

%% Coefficient perturbation NOW MEASURED UNDER THE PRICED OBJECTIVE:

%%   0.02 +4.110, 0.05 +4.029, 0.10 +2.960, 0.15 -3.863, 0.20 -8.125.

%%   This SUPERSEDES priced_design/, whose objective key was silently ignored by

%% Cross-environment check: NOT MEASURED. Earlier drafts cited a paired adapt

\item \textbf{The advantage is neither Pareto-dominant nor unconditional.} The
margin--violation front holds five of the fifteen controllers, and PPO
\cite{schulman2017} incurs $1330$ violation steps against the raw library's
$4130$ at $3.86$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$ less margin --- though two of those points are
one controller under two thresholds and a third rests partly on seven truncated
runs. The surrogate has no crop state, so terminal biomass is unpriced; the
out-of-distribution guard is a negative result (paired mean
$-0.69$~EUR\,m$^{-2}$, $+430$ violation steps); a $\pm15\,\%$ coefficient
perturbation costs $7.97$ on the mean against $3.35$ on the median
($p=4.3\times10^{-3}$); and all of it is simulation, one location, one planting
date, one computing environment.

\end{enumerate}

These findings establish a control-oriented model-selection result within the stated
virtual greenhouse. They do not demonstrate realised marketable yield, energy savings,
constraint safety or profitability in a physical facility. Before operational use, the
selected surrogate and controller require calibration against measured greenhouse
trajectories, hardware-in-the-loop testing and a crop-scale pilot that independently
records climate, actuator use, energy consumption and marketable yield.
